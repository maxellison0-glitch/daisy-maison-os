## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Custom Columns in Facebook Ads Manager — Seeing the Metrics That Actually Drive Decisions

**Callback:** In the Standard Events lesson (31 Aug), you learned that Facebook fires named events — ViewContent, AddToCart, Purchase — at each stage of the customer journey. Those events power the metrics in Ads Manager. But here is the problem: the default Ads Manager view does not show the metrics built FROM those events that actually tell you whether money is being well spent.

**What it is:** When you open Ads Manager, Facebook shows you a table of campaigns, ad sets, and ads. Each row has columns — and the DEFAULT columns are: Delivery, Bid Strategy, Budget, Amount Spent, Reach, Impressions, CPM, Link Clicks, CPC, CTR. Notice what is missing: Cost Per Purchase, Purchase ROAS, Frequency, and Conversion Rate. The default view shows you what Facebook wants you to care about — reach, impressions, clicks — metrics that make campaigns look active and busy. It hides the metrics that tell you whether you are making money.

**Why it works this way:** Facebook sells impressions. Its business model benefits when you think in impressions and clicks, because those always go up when you spend more. Cost Per Purchase and ROAS only go up when you spend WELL — and they sometimes tell you to spend less. Facebook has no incentive to put those front and centre.

**How to fix it:** In Ads Manager, click the "Columns" dropdown (top right of the data table), then "Customize Columns." This opens a searchable list of every metric Facebook tracks.

The must-add columns, in this order:

1. **Cost per Purchase** (search "cost per" then select "Website Purchases") — this is the CPP you see in this digest every morning. It is the single number that decides whether an ad set earns its budget or burns it.
2. **Purchases** (search "purchases" then "Website Purchases") — how many actual sales this campaign or ad set generated. Without this column, you see clicks but not conversions.
3. **Purchase ROAS** (search "ROAS" then "Website Purchase ROAS") — Facebook's own ROAS calculation using FB-attributed revenue. It differs from Shopify ROAS (which uses total_sales as the numerator), but it is useful for comparing campaigns WITHIN Facebook against each other.
4. **Frequency** (search "frequency") — the average number of times each person has seen your ad in the selected period. Below 2.0 per week is healthy for a TOF cold campaign. Above 3.0 means the audience is being recycled and fatigue is setting in. Above 5.0 and you are paying to annoy people.
5. **Reach** (search "reach") — unique people who saw your ad. If reach falls while spend stays flat, you are paying more to show the same ad to the same people. Reach and Frequency together tell you whether the audience pool is big enough for your budget.

Once you have added these five, click "Save as preset" at the bottom left and name it something like "Purchase View." From now on, every time you open Ads Manager, select this preset from the Columns dropdown and you see the real picture immediately, no external queries needed.

**Worked example with yesterday's data:** Looking at TOF-WEDDING in the default Ads Manager view, you would see:

| Ad Set | Spend | Clicks | CPC | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copy | £149.11 | 388 | £0.38 | 0.97% |
| more | £53.35 | 262 | £0.20 | 2.50% |

"more" looks better on CPC and CTR. But how much better? In the default view, you would close Ads Manager, pull Windsor data, calculate CPP manually, and compare. With the custom column preset, you would see this instead:

| Ad Set | Spend | Clicks | CPC | CTR | **Purchases** | **CPP** | **ROAS** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copy | £149.11 | 388 | £0.38 | 0.97% | **5** | **£29.82** | **0.81x** |
| more | £53.35 | 262 | £0.20 | 2.50% | **4** | **£13.34** | **2.45x** |

That one column — CPP — turns a "looks better" into "£16.48 per purchase cheaper, and the cheaper ad set actually generated more revenue." The decision to shift budget from Copy to more becomes obvious, immediate, and actionable — visible in the first second you open the screen, not after running a separate analytics query.

TikTok Ads Manager has the same concept: gear icon on the table header, then "Custom Columns." Add "Cost Per Conversion" and "Conversion Rate" — TikTok defaults are even more impression-heavy than Facebook's.
