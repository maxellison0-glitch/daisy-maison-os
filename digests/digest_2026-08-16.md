## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Placement Breakdown Reporting in Facebook Ads Manager

**Callback:** When we covered Facebook ad auction mechanics (15 Jun), we explained how the auction combines bid × estimated action rate × ad quality score to decide who wins each impression. Today's lesson answers the question that follows: WHERE do those auctions happen? Because not all placements are equal, and a campaign can look healthy at the aggregate while burning budget in low-converting placements.

**What a "placement" is.** Every time Facebook shows your ad, it appears on a specific surface inside the Meta ecosystem. Each surface has different user intent, different costs, and dramatically different conversion rates. The main placements are:

**Facebook Feed** — the main scrolling feed on desktop and mobile. This is the highest-intent placement for product ads. Someone scrolling their feed and pausing on a personalised wedding sign is in discovery mode, not gaming mode. Feed impressions cost more (higher CPM), but they produce the best conversion rates for e-commerce. When you see a Facebook CPC of £0.40–0.80, that's typically Feed-heavy delivery.

**Instagram Feed** — similar browsing intent to Facebook Feed, skewing younger and more visually driven. Good for product discovery, slightly cheaper than Facebook Feed. Instagram Stories and Reels are cheaper still — full-screen content that users swipe through quickly. Cheap impressions, fast scroll-past, lower purchase intent for considered purchases like personalised gifts.

**Audience Network** — this is the one that matters most and the one most people don't know about. Audience Network is Meta's ad network inside third-party apps and mobile games. It is by far the cheapest placement. Clicks on Audience Network are often accidental — someone tapping "close" on an interstitial ad in a puzzle game and hitting the ad instead. CPC can be £0.05–0.10, which looks brilliant in a blended report and is almost entirely worthless for converting gift purchases. When a campaign shows a blended CPC of £0.20–0.26 and you haven't set placement controls, a significant share of that cheap traffic is almost certainly Audience Network.

**Messenger** — ads in the Messenger inbox. Low volume, mixed quality, usually a rounding error in spend.

**Why this matters for Daisy Maison right now.** TOF-WEDDING spent £189.79 yesterday with 732 clicks at a blended CPC of £0.26. That is cheap. Suspiciously cheap. For context, the same campaign ran at £0.25 CPC on 3 Aug when it spent only £123.01 — the CPC stayed flat while spend grew 54%, which means the algorithm found extra cheap inventory somewhere. On a Highest Volume or Lowest Cost bidding strategy (the most common setup for TOF campaigns), Facebook optimises for the cheapest events. It will fill your budget by pouring into whichever placement offers the most clicks per pound — and that is almost always Audience Network and Instagram Stories, not Facebook Feed.

The worked example: £189.79 spend, 732 clicks, £0.26 blended CPC. Suppose the placement breakdown showed this split:

- Facebook Feed: £85 spend, 160 clicks, £0.53 CPC — the real converting traffic
- Instagram Feed: £42 spend, 115 clicks, £0.37 CPC — decent, some conversion potential
- Instagram Stories/Reels: £33 spend, 225 clicks, £0.15 CPC — cheap, mostly swipe-throughs
- Audience Network: £29.79 spend, 232 clicks, £0.13 CPC — almost certainly waste

In this scenario, 232 of 732 clicks (32%) came from people tapping ads in mobile games. They don't convert. They don't enter the pixel pool with genuine purchase intent. They dilute your remarketing audiences with non-buyers. And they make your campaign look more efficient than it is — because the headline says "732 clicks at £0.26" when the real figure for traffic that has any chance of converting is more like "275 clicks at £0.46" from Facebook and Instagram Feed.

**How to read the placement breakdown in Ads Manager:**

1. Log in to adsmanager.facebook.com
2. Find the campaign — TOF-WEDDING in this case
3. Click into the campaign to see the ad set level
4. Look for the **Breakdown** button in the toolbar above the data table (top-right area, near the date range selector)
5. Click it and select **By Delivery** → **Placement**
6. The data table now splits every row by placement: Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Instagram Stories, Instagram Reels, Audience Network, Messenger, and others
7. For each placement row you now see: Impressions, Reach, Clicks, CPC, CTR, Spend, and (if the pixel is firing) Results and Cost per Result

**What to look for:**
- If Audience Network has >15% of spend but near-zero conversions (Results column), it is waste
- Compare Feed CPC vs blended CPC — a large gap means cheap placements are dragging the average down and hiding the true cost of converting traffic
- If Stories/Reels show high impressions and clicks but the CTR looks inflated (>3%) with no Results, you're paying for accidental or idle engagement

**The fix (when the data confirms waste):** In the ad set settings, under Placements, switch from **Advantage+ Placements** (Meta's default, which means "we decide where to show it and we'll choose wherever is cheapest") to **Manual Placements**. Deselect Audience Network. Optionally deselect Messenger. Keep Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, and Stories/Reels. This immediately cuts the cheapest, worst-converting traffic and redirects that budget to placements where people actually buy things. CPC will rise. Clicks will fall. But conversion rate per click should improve — and that's what pays the bills.

On Google Ads, PMax does not allow placement-level control — you cannot split or exclude Search vs Shopping vs Display vs YouTube inside a PMax campaign. This is one of PMax's main limitations and frustrations. Google decides where your budget goes across all its properties. The closest diagnostic is the "Insights" tab inside the PMax campaign, which shows asset group performance and top search categories, but not placement-level spend.

On TikTok Ads (the likely third channel), placement is simpler — there's essentially one primary placement (the For You feed). But TikTok has its own audience network called **Pangle**. When creating a campaign, the equivalent decision is opting out of Pangle in the placement settings to avoid the same cheap-inventory trap.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Can you pull the placement breakdown for TOF-WEDDING for the last 7 days and screenshot it? I want to see what share of spend and clicks is going to Audience Network versus Facebook Feed and Instagram Feed." A good answer is a screenshot of the breakdown table with the placement column visible. An evasive answer is "Facebook optimises placements automatically, it knows best" — it does optimise, but it optimises for cheapness, not for gift purchases. The placement data tells you whether that optimisation is serving the store.

**You should now be able to:** Look at any campaign's CPC and ask "is this cheap because it's efficient, or cheap because it's buying junk placements?" — and know exactly where to find the placement breakdown to answer that question yourself.
