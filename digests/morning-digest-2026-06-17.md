## 📚 Lesson of the Day — Checkout Abandonment

*(For your eyes only — not for Daryl to see)*

**What it is**

Checkout abandonment is when someone adds a product to their cart, goes through to the checkout page, enters their details — and then leaves without paying. It's different from cart abandonment (where they add to cart and never even reach checkout). These two events happen at different stages of intent and for different reasons, and they need different fixes.

On the funnel, it looks like this:
- 220 sessions reached checkout yesterday
- 121 completed it
- That's **45% of people who started checkout not finishing it** — 99 sessions walked away at the last step

**Why it happens at checkout specifically**

When someone reaches checkout, they've already decided they want the product. Something changed their mind at the payment screen. The most common causes:

1. **Delivery anxiety** — they saw "3–5 working days" and weren't sure it would arrive before Father's Day. This is the most likely cause right now, in the final week before FD. They love the sign; they just don't believe it'll get there in time.

2. **Unexpected costs** — the shipping charge only appears at checkout. If someone mentally priced the product at £6.90 and then sees £2.99 postage added, even a small surprise creates friction. This is called "sticker shock" and it's one of the most studied causes of checkout abandonment in e-commerce.

3. **Distraction / phone interruption** — particularly on mobile. Someone is buying your sign, gets a WhatsApp message, and never comes back. This isn't fixable with design; it just happens.

4. **Payment friction** — they don't have their card to hand, preferred payment method isn't showing, Apple Pay didn't work. Small friction at payment = order lost.

**Why it matters at this exact moment**

With Father's Day 5 days away and delivery confidence peaking right now, abandonment reason #1 (delivery anxiety) is probably responsible for more of those 99 lost sessions than usual. The question is: how many of them would convert if they got a reminder with a clear "order by Thursday, guaranteed delivery by Sunday" message?

**What recovers it: the abandoned checkout email**

Shopify sends an automatic email to anyone who reaches checkout and enters their email but doesn't complete the purchase. By default it fires 1 hour after abandonment. You've already got this running.

This is **the highest-ROI automation in Shopify** — consistently. Industry benchmarks put recovery rates at 5–15% of abandoned checkouts. For your store, if 10% of yesterday's 99 abandoned checkouts came back after the email, that's ~10 extra orders and roughly £250 in revenue recovered — from a single automated email that cost nothing to send.

During Father's Day week specifically, that email should be doing real work. The subject line matters. If it currently says something generic like "You left something behind" — that's fine but not as good as "Your Dad's sign is ready — order by Thursday for Sunday delivery." The urgency and the reassurance in one line.

The lesson: ads bring people to checkout. The abandoned checkout email quietly recovers the people who almost bought. Watch the email open and recovery rates in Klaviyo or Shopify — that's where you'll see it working.
