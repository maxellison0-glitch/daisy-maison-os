## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Checkout breaks are ad problems too.** Both algorithms (Meta's and Google's PMax) learn only from purchases your pixel reports. When checkout stalls, each platform sees fewer buyers from its clicks and concludes its audience got worse. It then bids more cautiously or chases cheaper, lower-intent people. Yesterday Facebook recorded 12 purchases on £199.33 (£16.61 CPP), against 26 on £207.62 (£7.99) the day before. Its traffic held at 546 clicks (571 the day before), but its purchase data halved. A day or two of this is harmless, but a week of it resets what "good" looks like for both platforms. That is why a checkout fault must be fixed before any budget call. TikTok's pixel learns the same way. **Ask Daryl this:** "When store-side conversion drops for a day, what do you do in the accounts, and when would you exclude those dates from bidding?" A good answer mentions Google's data-exclusion feature for tracking or site outages; an evasive one is "the algorithm will sort it out." **You should now be able to** tell a site problem from an ads problem by checking where the funnel broke before touching a budget.
