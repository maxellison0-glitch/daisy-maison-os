## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential — for Max only, not Daryl)*

**Price competitiveness & query intent in Google Shopping — why a "surging" search term can be worthless.**

Today's tracker shows `teacher gifts` pulling **76.4 impressions/day** (up 4x from the June baseline) and `personalised teacher gifts` at **50.5/day** — and both have produced **zero sales on just 14 clicks across 12 days**. It's tempting to celebrate the impression growth or throw bid at it. Don't. Here's what's actually happening.

In Google Shopping, your product card shows **with its price, right next to competitors' cards for the same search**. Google isn't matching keywords — it's matching the *query* to your product feed and then letting the shopper compare on image and price in the carousel. Two things determine whether an impression turns into a click and then a sale:

1. **Query intent.** "teacher gifts" is a broad *head* term. The people typing it are often browsing for cheap, generic gifts — a £6 mug, a Lego set (note the June baseline is full of "lego teacher gift", "teacher lego"). Our £15–30 personalised pieces show up, get seen, but don't get the click because the intent behind the query is mismatched. High impressions on a broad term is **reach vanity**, not demand.
2. **Price competitiveness.** Google literally scores how your price compares to others showing for that query (you can see this as "price competitiveness" in Merchant Center). If a shopper sees a £4.99 keyring next to our £15 one, we lose the click on price even when ours is better made.

The diagnostic skill: read a term by its **full funnel**, not one number. `teacher gifts` = high impressions, tiny CTR (0.5%), zero conversions → the problem is **downstream of the ad** (query intent + carousel price), so the fix is *not* a higher bid. The right moves are either (a) add it as a **negative keyword** if it's just burning reach, or (b) accept it as free brand exposure and do nothing — but never pay more for it. Contrast that with wedding's converting terms (`wedding gift ideas`, `wedding gifts for couples`) where impressions *and* conversions both move — that's real demand worth defending.

This is exactly the kind of judgement you'll make yourself when the account comes in-house: the search-terms report tells you *where* a problem sits in the funnel, and where it sits tells you which lever to pull. An agency that "optimised" `teacher gifts` by raising its bid would be lighting money on fire.
