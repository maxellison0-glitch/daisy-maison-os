## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**MER: our "ROAS" is really a blended number, and an email day inflates it**

*Builds on Incrementality (7 Jul).* Platform ROAS is the revenue Facebook or Google *claim*, divided by their spend. The digest uses something else: **MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio)**, which is all Shopify revenue ÷ all ad spend. MER can't be gamed by attribution. The catch is the top line: it includes every sale, including sales that no ad bought (email, word of mouth, returning buyers). So on a day you send an email, MER rises even if the ads did nothing differently.

**Worked:** £1,301.29 ÷ £428.52 = **3.04x**. Direct completed checkouts were 12, vs a 7-day average of 7.0, so the email added ~5 orders × £29.58 ≈ £148 (estimate). (£1,301.29 − £148) ÷ £428.52 = **2.69x**, which is red. Read an email day's MER against the *ad-only* baseline before scaling.

**TikTok:** same logic. TikTok-attributed ROAS will overclaim; judge it on MER.

**Ask Daryl this:** "When you judge scaling, do you use platform ROAS or our Shopify MER, and how do you strip out email days?" A good answer names MER and a baseline. An evasive one quotes Google's in-platform ROAS.

**You should now be able to** tell whether a green day was bought by ads or by an email.
