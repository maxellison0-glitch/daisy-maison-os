## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Purchaser exclusions: keeping a prospecting campaign honest**

*This builds on Incrementality (attributed vs caused sales).* An **exclusion** tells Meta never to show an ad set to a named audience. The usual one is **"Purchase 30"**: people the Pixel (Meta's tracking code on the site) saw buy in the last 30 days. Why it matters: a purchase-optimised campaign hunts for whoever is most likely to buy. Recent buyers and recent visitors score highest, so an unexcluded "cold" (TOF, top-of-funnel) campaign drifts into retargeting. It then claims credit for sales that would have happened anyway. From the outside you see a CPP that looks great while the share of new customers in Shopify falls. The cost of excluding is a smaller pool and a slightly higher CPP. That's real money, not waste, and it's why exclusions get relaxed before Christmas, when people who bought once often buy again.

**Worked example (16 Oct):** your Reel excludes Purchase 30, CPP £11.19. TOF-WEDDING shows £8.13. Shopify: 26 new + 3 returning = 29. Even if all 3 returning buyers sat inside Facebook's 16 claimed purchases, at least 13 of the 16 were new (81%+).

**Ask Daryl this:** "Do TOF-WEDDING and Social Engagement exclude recent purchasers, and which window?" Good answer: names the audience and the days. Evasive answer: "Advantage+ handles it."

**TikTok:** has the same custom-audience exclusions.

**You should now be able to** tell whether two CPPs are actually comparable before you call a winner.
