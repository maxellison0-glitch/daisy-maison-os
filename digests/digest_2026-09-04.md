## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Ad Set Portfolio Evaluation — Reading Cost Per Purchase to Judge Where Budget Is Working

**CALLBACK:** On 1 Sep you learned Ad Set Budget Reallocation — moving money from expensive ad sets to cheaper ones using CPP as the deciding metric. Today builds on that: before you can reallocate, you need to be able to *read the entire portfolio* of ad sets across all campaigns at once, rank them by CPP, and spot the patterns that tell you which ad sets deserve more budget, which deserve less, and which are too new to judge.

**What it is.** When you open Ads Manager and look at your campaigns, each campaign contains one or more ad sets, each with its own audience, creative, and budget. An ad set portfolio evaluation means pulling every active ad set into one view, sorting by Cost Per Purchase (CPP = ad set spend ÷ purchases), and treating the result like a league table. The position in that table — not the campaign it sits inside — determines where money should flow.

**Why campaign-level ROAS misleads.** A campaign's overall ROAS blends all its ad sets together. TOF-WEDDING yesterday showed £176.59 spend and 18 FB-attributed purchases — looks efficient. But inside it, TOF - MAIN spent £126.15 for 14 purchases (£9.01 CPP) while "more" spent £38.17 for just 2 purchases (£19.09 CPP). The campaign average hides that one ad set is converting at more than double the cost of another. If you judged at campaign level, you would keep both running identically. The ad set view forces the real question: should the £38 that went to "more" have gone to TOF - MAIN instead?

**The three-tier ranking system.** Once you have every ad set's CPP in front of you, sort ascending (cheapest first) and classify:

1. **Winners** (CPP below your break-even CPA): these earn their budget and can absorb more. Your break-even CPA at 60% gross margin and £29.22 AOV is roughly £17.53 (£29.22 × 0.60). Any ad set below £17.53 CPP is profitable before fixed costs.

2. **Marginal** (CPP between break-even and 1.5× break-even, i.e. £17.53–£26.30): generating purchases but at a cost that leaves thin margin. Hold budget, don't scale.

3. **Losers** (CPP above 1.5× break-even, i.e. above £26.30): burning cash. Cut budget or pause unless in learning phase (<50 purchases lifetime).

**Yesterday's portfolio ranked by CPP:**

| Rank | Ad Set | Campaign | Spend | Purchases | CPP | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New Sales – Copy | Social Engagement | £9.95 | 3 | £3.32 | Winner |
| 2 | test_creatives_2 | TOF-WEDDING | £4.32 | 1 | £4.32 | Too new |
| 3 | New Sales | Social Engagement | £13.11 | 2 | £6.56 | Winner |
| 4 | test_creatives | TOF-WEDDING | £7.95 | 1 | £7.95 | Too new |
| 5 | TOF - MAIN | TOF-WEDDING | £126.15 | 14 | £9.01 | Winner |
| 6 | more | TOF-WEDDING | £38.17 | 2 | £19.09 | Marginal |

Five of six ad sets are below break-even. "More" is the only marginal performer, and even that would have been a winner on 31 Aug (£10.53 CPP). One bad day doesn't make it a loser — but two consecutive marginal days (£22.32 on 2 Sep, £19.09 yesterday) means it's trending in the wrong direction.

**The "too new to judge" rule.** The test ad sets both show spectacular CPPs (£4.32 and £7.95), which might make you want to scale them immediately. This is a trap. One purchase each on £12.27 combined spend is statistically meaningless — it takes roughly 15–20 purchases before an ad set's CPP stabilises. At their current daily spend rate (£4–8/day), they won't reach that threshold for 10–15 days. Until then, treat their CPP as "unknown", not "excellent". The arithmetic is seductive but the sample is noise.

**How to do this yourself.** In Ads Manager:

1. Set the custom columns preset you built on 2 Sep (Cost Per Purchase, Purchases, Purchase ROAS, Frequency, Reach).
2. Click the campaign row to expand it, showing all ad sets underneath.
3. Set the date range to "Yesterday" (or "Last 7 days" for a rolling view).
4. Read straight down the Cost Per Purchase column, ignoring which campaign each ad set belongs to.
5. Rank them mentally: cheapest CPP first.
6. Any ad set above £26 CPP for 3+ consecutive days → cut its budget by 20–30%.
7. Any ad set below £10 CPP for 5+ consecutive days → candidate for a 20% budget increase.

**Worked example with yesterday's numbers.** You have £199.65 of Facebook budget yesterday. The optimal allocation (in hindsight) would concentrate spend on the three proven winners: TOF - MAIN (£9.01 CPP), New Sales – Copy (£3.32 CPP), and New Sales (£6.56 CPP). Their combined £149.21 spend yesterday produced 19 purchases. The remaining £50.44 went to "more" (2 purchases), test_creatives (1), and test_creatives_2 (1). If that £38.17 from "more" had gone to TOF - MAIN instead (holding its CPP constant), that buys ~4 additional purchases → 23 total instead of 23, or 22 total from Facebook instead of the actual 23. The marginal gain from reallocation is small because TOF - MAIN is already dominant. The bigger leverage is the test ad sets: if either proves itself over 2 weeks, a £30–50/day budget there could produce 4–6 purchases/day at £7–8 CPP — cheaper than anything currently running at scale.

**TikTok note:** TikTok's equivalent view is Campaign → Ad Group (= ad set) → sort by Cost Per Conversion. The same three-tier ranking applies, but TikTok audiences fatigue 3–5× faster than Facebook, so "3+ consecutive days above break-even" becomes "2+ consecutive days" before cutting.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "What creative is running in `test_creatives` and `test_creatives_2` — is it new static images, video, or carousel? And what audience are they targeting — same broad audience as TOF - MAIN, or a narrower test?" A good answer names the specific creative format and audience. An evasive answer is "just testing some things" without naming what variable changed — that means there is no structured test, just random creative swaps.

**You should now be able to:** open Ads Manager, sort all active ad sets by Cost Per Purchase regardless of which campaign they sit in, classify each as Winner / Marginal / Loser / Too New, and decide where the next budget shift should go — without waiting for the morning digest to tell you.
