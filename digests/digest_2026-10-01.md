## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Google's daily budget is an average, not a ceiling: the 2× rule and the monthly charge limit.** This builds on budget pacing (15 Jul) and budget utilisation rate.

- **The rule.** On Facebook, a £75 daily budget means roughly £75 a day. On Google, the daily budget is a *monthly average*. Google can spend **up to 2× the daily budget on any single day** when it predicts better-converting traffic.
- **The monthly charge limit.** You are never billed more than **30.4 × the daily budget** in a calendar month. 30.4 is the average number of days in a month.
- **Why it works this way.** Search demand is lumpy. Google would rather overspend on a heavy-search day and underspend on a quiet one than spend evenly and miss the busy day.
- **What it looks like from outside.** A spike up to 2× the normal day, followed by lighter days, with no change visible in the account.
- **How it goes wrong.** Google's "better traffic" prediction can be wrong. The overdelivery day buys extra auctions at higher bids, so the CPC rises and the marginal clicks are the worst of the day. A real budget raise looks the same on day one, but it persists.

**Worked example (30 Sep):**
1. Google spent £592.93 against a 24–29 Sep average of £301.24. That is 1.97×, almost exactly on the 2× line.
2. The extra £234.30 bought 118 extra clicks (664 − 546), so each marginal click cost £1.99, against an average CPC of £0.89.
3. If the budget is still ~£300, the October monthly cap is £300 × 30.4 = £9,120 (~£300/day average), so Google must spend less on other days to hold that average.
4. If spend stays near £600, the budget was raised. The cost runs at about £8,900 a month more than before (£291.69 × 30.4), at a 1.37x marginal ROAS.

You can tell which by tomorrow. Windsor doesn't return Google budgets, so the day-after spend pattern is your only signal from here.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Did PMAX AUG's budget change on 30 Sep, or was that 2× overdelivery? If it was raised, what marginal ROAS justified it?" A good answer gives a yes or no with the budget figure. An evasive one is "Google's just finding more volume."

TikTok's daily budgets are hard caps, closer to Facebook than to Google.

**You should now be able to** tell a Google overdelivery day from a budget raise by reading the next day's spend, and work out the monthly bill from any daily budget.
