## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### The Emergency Stop — When to Pause Campaigns and How

**Callback:** On 13 Jun you learned the break-even ROAS formula: 1 ÷ gross margin = the ROAS below which every ad-attributed order loses money. At ~60% gross margin, break-even is ~1.67x. On 23 Jun you learned budget scaling rules — the discipline of waiting for 3–5 consecutive green days before adding spend. Today is the inverse: the discipline of cutting when ROAS drops below break-even, before the store bleeds money into a trough it cannot buy its way out of.

**What it is.** An emergency stop is a deliberate, same-morning decision to pause or sharply cut ad campaigns when the store's blended ROAS drops below break-even. It is not a panic reaction — it is the logical consequence of the break-even formula. Below 1.67x, every pound of ad spend produces less gross profit than it costs. The store is paying to lose money. The correct response is not to wait and hope tomorrow is better. The correct response is to stop the bleed immediately and diagnose the cause from a position of zero additional waste.

**Why it matters.** The instinct when sales drop is to spend more — "if I just push harder, the orders will come." This is the single most expensive mistake an in-house operator can make. Facebook and Google are auction platforms. When demand drops — fewer people searching for "personalised wedding gift," fewer high-intent users on social — the auction does not get cheaper. It gets more expensive, because the same number of advertisers are bidding for fewer buyers. Spending into a demand trough buys the worst available clicks at the highest available prices. The store does not need ads to survive a single bad day. What it needs is to not burn £630 acquiring 34 orders when some portion of those orders would have arrived organically at a cost of zero.

**The mechanism.** The emergency stop is not "turn everything off." It is a tiered protocol:

1. **First, diagnose.** Is the drop spend-caused (you spent more and got worse returns — diminishing returns), demand-caused (sessions and search volume dropped broadly — the market shrank), traffic-quality-caused (clicks are arriving but not converting — you are buying the wrong people), or site-caused (CVR collapsed while traffic stayed normal)? Each diagnosis has a different fix. Yesterday's data says demand-caused: sessions dropped 25% (825 vs 1,107), every traffic source lost absolute volume, cart-add rate stayed normal at 8.8%. The buyers were simply not there.

2. **Then, triage campaigns.** Not all campaigns are equal. PMAX CSS DEC captures existing Google search demand — when demand drops, it has fewer good auctions to bid on, so CPC rises (£0.90 yesterday). Cutting its budget to £280 removes the expensive marginal clicks but keeps the campaign alive for when demand recovers. EOI-Multiple produces nothing measurable — pause outright. TOF-WEDDING is a Facebook discovery campaign — when it cannot find high-intent users, it buys low-intent ones, dragging blended ROAS down. Cap at £125.

3. **Set a recovery condition.** An emergency stop is not permanent. State the condition for restarting before you cut: "if Friday's ROAS exceeds 2.0x on reduced spend, hold reduced budgets through the weekend and reassess Monday." Without a stated condition, paused campaigns stay paused indefinitely, and the store loses the audience-building investment it has already paid for. A campaign paused for a week loses some of its learned targeting data; one paused for a day loses almost nothing.

**Where you find the controls.** In Google Ads, a campaign's daily budget is on the campaign row — click the pencil icon next to the budget figure, type the new number, save. Changes take effect within the hour. In Facebook Ads Manager, the daily budget is in the ad set's Budget & Schedule section (if ABO) or the campaign level (if CBO). The on/off toggle on any campaign or ad set row is the pause button. A paused campaign retains all its learning data and audience history — it does not reset when restarted. TikTok Ads Manager has an identical toggle and the same behaviour: pausing preserves learning.

**Worked example.** Yesterday: £629.57 total spend, £972.20 total sales, 1.54x ROAS. Break-even at ~60% margin is 1.67x.

At 1.54x, each £1 of ad spend generates £1.54 of revenue. Gross profit on that revenue at 60% margin: £0.92. Cost of the ad pound: £1.00. **Net loss per ad pound: −£0.08.** Over £630 of spend: **the store lost approximately £50 in gross profit** just from running ads that day. If the recommended cuts had been in place (PMAX at £280, EOI-Multiple paused, TOF-WEDDING at £125), spend would have been ~£536. Even at the same 1.54x, the loss would have been £43 — smaller, and at lower spend ROAS typically recovers because the cheapest clicks are served first.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Can you set PMAX CSS DEC to £280/day and pause EOI-Multiple today, and screenshot the campaign-level daily budget so I can see what it's actually set to versus what it spends?" A good answer is a screenshot plus confirmation. An evasive answer is "the algorithm knows best" or "we should wait for more data" — those are the exact phrases that cost £1,130 in ignored recommendations over the last ten days.

**You should now be able to** look at a day's blended ROAS, compare it to the 1.67x break-even line, and decide the same morning whether to cut or hold — and know which campaigns to cut first, which to preserve, and what condition triggers a restart.
