## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Branded vs Non-Branded Search

**Building on:** On 3 Jul you learned how to read Google's search terms report — the actual queries shoppers type that trigger your Shopping ads. On 13 Jul you learned how to tell a valuable query from a vanity one (high impressions but zero conversions = wrong audience intent). Today adds a layer on top of both: before you judge any individual search term, you need to classify it. Every query that triggers one of your ads falls into one of two buckets, and the bucket tells you what the searcher already knows about you — which changes everything about what the click is worth.

**The concept.** A *branded* search is any query that contains your business name: "daisy maison", "daisy maison uk", "daisy maison wedding sign". A *non-branded* search is everything else: "personalised wedding gift", "mr and mrs sign", "teacher gifts uk". The distinction matters because branded and non-branded searchers are in fundamentally different states of mind, and they cost different amounts to acquire.

A branded searcher already knows you exist. They might have seen a Facebook ad last week, been sent a product link in a WhatsApp group, or bought from you at Christmas. They have already decided to come to *your* store. The Google click is almost free in auction terms because no competitor is bidding on "daisy maison" — there is no competition for your own name. And the conversion rate on branded clicks is typically three to five times higher than non-branded, because the intent is already resolved: they know what they want and where to get it.

A non-branded searcher does not know you. They typed "personalised wedding gift" because they want one, and Google is showing them your product card in a carousel alongside ten competing stores. You are one option in a lineup. The click costs more because every gift retailer in the UK is bidding on the same query. And the conversion rate is lower because the shopper is still comparing — your price, your photos, your reviews, your delivery promise all have to win against the cards beside yours in the same row.

**Why this matters for reading your numbers.** If most of your Google revenue comes from branded search, your Google Ads are not *creating* demand — they are *capturing* demand that something else created (usually Facebook, word of mouth, or repeat buyers who Google your name instead of typing the URL). The ads are still worth running — you do not want a competitor bidding on "daisy maison" and stealing the click — but scaling Google spend will not scale revenue, because the branded pool is fixed. You cannot make more people search "daisy maison" by bidding higher on Google.

If most of your Google revenue comes from non-branded search, your ads ARE creating demand — or more precisely, capturing intent that would otherwise go to a competitor. That revenue is scalable: there will always be more people searching "personalised wedding gift" than searching for you by name, and a better title or a higher budget puts you in front of more of them.

**The mechanism.** Google's search terms report (which Daryl can pull from the Ads dashboard, or which Windsor surfaces in the title optimisation tracker data) shows you every query that triggered a click. You classify each one: does it contain "daisy", "daisy maison", or your shop name? Branded. Everything else? Non-branded. Then you sum the impressions, clicks, and revenue for each bucket. The ratio tells you where your Google money is really working — and whether scaling that spend will find new customers or just re-acquire existing ones at a higher cost.

**Worked example with yesterday's numbers.** Google delivered **622 clicks** yesterday (PMAX CSS DEC 502 + All Other CSS 120) at a combined **£416.09 spend**. Those 622 clicks all came from search queries. Some were "daisy maison wedding sign" — branded, the shopper already knew you. Others were "personalised wedding gift uk" — non-branded, the shopper was discovering you for the first time through a product card.

You cannot split yesterday's 622 clicks into branded vs non-branded from the daily pull alone (that requires the full search terms report). But the title optimisation baseline gives a directional read: the June baseline for wedding non-branded terms — "wedding gifts" at 680 imp/day, "personalised wedding gifts" at 230 imp/day, "wedding gift ideas" at 335 imp/day — totalled roughly 20,755 impressions per day and 246 clicks per day across all tracked wedding terms. That is the non-branded discovery engine — people searching for *what you sell*, not *who you are*. At yesterday's £416 Google spend and 622 clicks, if even half of those clicks are non-branded, that is ~310 clicks of genuine demand capture at roughly £1.34 each. Branded clicks, by contrast, might cost £0.10–0.20 each — the price difference is the cost of reaching someone who does not yet know you exist versus someone who already does.

**Why this matters right now.** Search was the #1 traffic source yesterday at **46.9% (471 sessions)**, the highest search share in weeks and continuing a trend that started with the 1 Jul title rewrite. Better titles match more non-branded queries, which drives more discovery traffic through Google Shopping. The question for Daryl is what share of those 471 search sessions were branded (people who already knew Daisy Maison) versus non-branded (people discovering it for the first time through a Shopping card). That ratio is the quality check on whether the title changes are actually widening the funnel — bringing in new customers — or whether the extra search traffic is just existing customers clicking through Google instead of typing the URL directly.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Can you pull the search terms report for the last seven days and split the clicks and conversions into branded — anything containing 'daisy maison' — versus non-branded? I want to know what percentage of our Google revenue is people who already know us versus people finding us for the first time." A good answer gives you two clear numbers and a ratio. An evasive answer will talk about "campaign performance" or "overall ROAS" without breaking the queries down — that usually means they have not looked, or the branded share is uncomfortably high, meaning the ads are recapturing your own customers rather than finding new ones.

**You should now be able to:** look at any day's Google traffic number and ask the right diagnostic question — is this branded traffic we would get anyway, or non-branded discovery that the ads and titles are earning?
