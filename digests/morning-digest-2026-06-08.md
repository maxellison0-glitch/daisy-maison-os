## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Facebook Campaign Structure — What TOF, [DTD], and [RDD] Actually Mean

This one has been hanging in the background since the first lesson, and now that you know the PMax layer on Google (from yesterday's lesson), it's time to map the same idea across to Facebook. Once you understand how your Facebook account is structured, Daryl's campaign-level updates will immediately make more sense.

**The three-level structure**

Every Facebook ad account is organised the same way: **Campaign → Ad Set → Ad**. A Campaign holds the objective (what you're optimising for — in this case, purchases) and the top-level budget. Inside each Campaign there are Ad Sets, which define the audience, the placement (Facebook feed, Instagram, Messenger, etc.) and the budget schedule. Inside each Ad Set there are Ads — the actual image or video and the copy the customer sees. This isn't just admin labelling — it matters because Facebook's algorithm optimises *within* Ad Sets, not across them. If you've got five ads inside one Ad Set, Facebook will automatically push more budget to whichever ad is performing best. Each level controls something specific, and changing something at the wrong level has different consequences.

**TOF — Top of Funnel (cold audience)**

TOF campaigns target people who have never encountered Daisy Maison before. Facebook uses broad targeting or interest-based audiences to find them — things like people who have liked personalised gift pages, homeware brands, or who match a broad demographic that overlaps with your buyers. The goal here is not immediate conversion. It's getting the brand in front of strangers and starting to warm them up. TOF typically has the lowest ROAS of any layer — converting a cold stranger is hard — but it's essential because without it, the warmer layers below run out of fresh audiences to retarget. Think of it as the tap that fills the funnel. If TOF goes dark, retargeting dries up 1–3 weeks later.

**[DTD] — Dynamic Retargeting (warm audience)**

DTD is the mid-funnel retargeting layer. It targets people who have already been to the website but haven't purchased. Facebook uses the Pixel (a piece of tracking code installed on your site) plus your product catalogue to automatically build personalised ads for each visitor. If someone spent 3 minutes on the Football Stadium Street Sign page and left, Facebook dynamically generates an ad showing them that exact product — not a generic Daisy Maison ad. This is called a Dynamic Product Ad (DPA). "Dynamic" means the creative is assembled automatically by Facebook from your live Shopify catalogue, so you don't design individual ads — you set up the template once and it does the work. DTD is typically the highest-ROAS layer in any account precisely because it's reaching people who already showed intent.

**[RDD] — CSS-enhanced Remarketing**

[RDD] is the layer that connects to the CSS (Comparison Shopping Service) partner — the same CSS discussed in the 4 June lesson, which gives a ~20% click advantage in Google Shopping. In the Facebook context, [RDD] applies a similar enhanced-placement mechanic but within the Facebook/Instagram ecosystem. The key unresolved question about your account remains: who controls the CSS contract behind [RDD]? Is it a third party that Daryl has sub-contracted, or is it Daryl's own agency infrastructure? This distinction is critical if you ever move ads in-house, because it determines whether [RDD] can continue running or needs to be rebuilt with a new CSS provider.

**Why this matters for yesterday's 788 Facebook sessions**

Facebook drove 788 sessions yesterday — 45% of all your traffic. That traffic isn't monolithic. Some of those 788 people are complete strangers seeing Daisy Maison for the first time (TOF). Some are people who came to the site last week and are being followed up with the exact product they viewed (DTD). Some are in the CSS-enhanced remarketing bucket ([RDD]). Understanding this split tells you where the problems are if performance softens. If TOF spend is up but sessions from new visitors are flat, the creative may be stale. If DTD spend is up but conversion is dropping, there may be a Pixel or catalogue issue. Daryl's updates will start using these terms — you can now read them with context rather than nodding along.
