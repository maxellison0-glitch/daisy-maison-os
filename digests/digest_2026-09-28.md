## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Taking CPP apart: CPM × CTR × conversion.** This builds on CPC vs CPM and on creative fatigue. Cost per purchase (CPP) is not one number. It is three dials multiplied together: **CPP = CPM ÷ (1,000 × CTR × click-to-purchase rate)**. CPM is what the auction charges for 1,000 views, CTR is the share of viewers who click, and click-to-purchase is the share of clickers who buy. For TOF-WEDDING on Saturday, £4.51 ÷ (1,000 × 1.29% × 3.04%) = £11.50. On Sunday, £4.50 ÷ (1,000 × 1.02% × 3.21%) = £13.74. CPM was flat and conversion actually improved, so the whole rise in actual CPP (£11.49 → £13.70) came from CTR. The extra £49 of budget went to people who don't stop scrolling for the ad. That points to the creative and the reach of the audience, not to the market (CPM) or the website (conversion), so the fix is fresh creative, not a budget cut or a site change. TikTok breaks down the same way (CPM × CTR × CVR). **Ask Daryl this:** "TOF-WEDDING's CTR went from 1.29% to 1.02% the day after the £200 raise while CPM held. Is that the audience widening, or TOF-MAIN's creative wearing out, and what are you rotating in?" A good answer names the creative or the frequency. An evasive one says "it needs time to settle". **You should now be able to** take any CPP rise apart into auction, creative and site, and name which one moved.
