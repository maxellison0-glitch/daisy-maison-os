## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Creative Fatigue vs Audience Exhaustion — How to Tell Which One Is Killing Your Ad

**Callback:** In June (Lesson 12, Ad Frequency and Audience Saturation), you learned that seasonal campaigns burn out because the high-intent audience pool shrinks — Facebook runs out of people to show the ad to. Today's lesson covers the *other* reason ads decline: the creative itself goes stale. Both look the same from the outside (worse results over time), but they have different causes, different diagnostic signals, and critically, different fixes. Applying the wrong fix wastes money and time.

**The two failure modes.** When a campaign that was performing well starts to degrade, one of two things has gone wrong:

1. **Audience exhaustion** means you have shown your ad to every person worth showing it to. The *audience pool* is the constraint. Facebook has to bid harder to find the remaining untapped users, or it starts showing the ad to people further and further outside your ideal customer profile. You see this when CPM (cost per thousand impressions — what you pay for a thousand pairs of eyes) rises sharply, reach declines, and frequency climbs above 3–4x in a 7-day window. The fix is on the *audience* side: broaden your targeting, test a new lookalike audience with a wider percentage, or accept that the demand pool for this particular audience is spent.

2. **Creative fatigue** means the audience is still there — Facebook can find new people cheaply — but the *ad itself* has become invisible. People who have seen it once or twice have trained their thumbs to scroll past it. It is like a billboard on your commute: after the first week you stop reading it, even though it is right there every morning. CPM stays flat or even drops (plenty of cheap inventory available), but CTR (click-through rate — the percentage of people who see the ad and actually click) falls. The fix is on the *creative* side: add a new ad into the same ad set. Crucially, you do NOT edit the existing ad — editing resets the learning phase (Lesson 4, 8 Jun) and destroys all the accumulated auction data. You add a *new* ad alongside the old one, and Facebook's algorithm will naturally shift delivery toward whichever gets better engagement.

**How to tell the difference in Ads Manager.** The diagnostic is a two-column check you can do in thirty seconds once you know what to look for:

- **CPM rising + CTR stable or rising = audience exhaustion.** Facebook is paying more per impression because the cheap, high-intent slice of the audience is used up. But when it does reach someone new, that person still clicks — the creative is fine. Fix: expand or replace the audience.

- **CPM flat or falling + CTR declining = creative fatigue.** Facebook is finding impressions cheaply (the audience is plentiful), but fewer people bother clicking. The creative has lost its power to stop the scroll. Fix: new creative.

- **CPM rising AND CTR falling = both.** The worst case. The audience is shrinking AND the ad is stale. This usually happens after 3–4 weeks of sustained high spend with no creative refresh. Fix: new creative AND new or expanded audience.

Where to find these numbers: in Ads Manager, select the ad set, set the date range to the last 7 days, and make sure CPM and CTR columns are visible (Columns → Customise Columns → search "CPM" and "CTR (link click-through rate)"). If you set up a saved column preset with Spend, CPC, CTR, CPM, Frequency, Results, and Cost Per Result, you can run this diagnostic in the time it takes to scroll one row.

**Worked example: TOF-WEDDING, 3 Aug vs 17 Aug.**

On 3 August, TOF-WEDDING spent £123.01, generated 492 clicks at a 1.60% CTR. Working backwards from the CPC (£0.25) and CTR, the implied impressions were about 30,750, giving a CPM of £123.01 ÷ 30.75 = **£4.00**.

Yesterday (17 August), TOF-WEDDING spent £173.29, generated 649 clicks at a 1.42% CTR on 45,708 impressions, giving a CPM of £173.29 ÷ 45.708 = **£3.79**.

Over two weeks of elevated spend:
- CPM went *down* 5.3% (£4.00 → £3.79) — plenty of audience available, no sign of exhaustion
- CTR went *down* 11.3% (1.60% → 1.42%) — fewer people clicking per impression
- CPC went *up* 8.0% (£0.25 → £0.27) — paying more per click because each click takes more impressions to generate

This is the textbook creative fatigue pattern. Facebook is finding cheap impressions (CPM down), but people are scrolling past the ad (CTR down). The audience is not the problem. The creative is going stale. The CPC rise is a mechanical consequence: if it takes more impressions to generate each click, each click costs more even though each impression is cheap.

The decay is still mild — an 11% CTR decline over two weeks is early-stage fatigue, not a collapse — so there is time to brief and build new creative before it gets critical. But creative fatigue does not reverse itself. Once people start scrolling past, they do not start reading again.

**On TikTok Ads** (the likely third channel): TikTok creative fatigue is far more aggressive — ads can wear out in 3–5 days versus 2–4 weeks on Facebook, because the For You Page shows content more frequently and users expect constant novelty. TikTok advertisers typically run 3–5 active creatives per ad group and rotate new ones in weekly.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "What is the current 7-day frequency on the TOF-WEDDING ad set, and when was the last time you added new creative into it?" A good answer is a specific frequency number (ideally under 2.5 for a TOF campaign) and a date. An evasive answer is "it's fine" or "I'll look into it." If frequency is above 3.0 and the last creative refresh was more than 14 days ago, the answer is clear: add new creative this week.

**You should now be able to:** look at a campaign that is performing worse than last week, pull up CPM and CTR in Ads Manager, and diagnose whether the problem is the audience or the creative — and know that the two problems require completely different fixes.
