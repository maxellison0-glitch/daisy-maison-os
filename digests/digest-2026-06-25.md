## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### The "Patience Budget": Why Cold Campaigns Look Broken Before They Work

Yesterday, SALES TEACHER spent £60 and got 349 clicks. No teacher product appeared in the top sellers. If you looked at that in isolation, you'd pull the campaign immediately. That would be a mistake — and understanding why is one of the more counterintuitive things in paid advertising.

**What's actually happening in those first days**

When you launch a brand-new campaign to a cold audience — people who've never heard of Daisy Maison, haven't visited the site, and haven't bought — the Meta algorithm has nothing to go on. It doesn't know who in your target audience is actually going to buy. So it starts by casting a wide net across your defined audience (in this case, people likely to be interested in teacher gifts) and observing who clicks.

In those first few days, the algorithm is running what you can think of as a live experiment: "out of all these people who clicked, which types of people actually ended up purchasing?" It watches for the pixel firing on your order confirmation page. Every purchase that completes gives it a data point: the buyer's age, gender, time of day, device, interests, location. Every non-purchase gives it the opposite signal.

The critical threshold Meta needs is **roughly 50 purchase events** before it has enough signal to stop guessing and start optimising properly. Until that point, it's in the learning phase — spending your budget to gather data, not to drive efficient conversions. Clicks will be cheap. Conversions will be rare or zero.

**Why 349 clicks at £0.17 CPC is actually a good sign**

Cheap CPC means the audience is responsive to the creative — people are clicking. That's Step 1. The job now is to see whether those clicks convert at a meaningful rate once the algorithm starts identifying the right sub-group within the audience. If CPC were high (say £0.80+) AND there were no conversions, that would be a double warning. But cheap clicks + low early conversions is the normal shape of a healthy cold campaign.

**How much runway before you judge it?**

A reasonable rule of thumb: don't evaluate a cold campaign for ROAS until it's had at least 7 days and at least 100 clicks. Yesterday was one day with 349 clicks — way past the click threshold in a single day. But ROAS needs a week of data, not 24 hours. The algorithm needs to learn from a meaningful number of purchase events (or near-purchase events — people who added to cart, reached checkout).

**Applied to your account right now**

Here's the concrete version: SALES TEACHER has been running since around 19–20 June. That's roughly 5 days. During those 5 days, it's accumulated clicks across Facebook. The question is: how many purchases has the pixel recorded from teacher-traffic sessions? If it's fewer than 10, the algorithm is still guessing. If it's 20–30, it's starting to narrow in. If teacher products start appearing in the daily top 8 in the next 2–3 days, that's the algorithm finding its footing.

What you'd want to see by day 10–14: either teacher products in the top sellers (conversion signal) or a clear pattern where the clicks are cheap but add-to-cart rate is also low (which would suggest audience or product mismatch rather than just a cold start).

**The number to watch**

When teacher products start appearing in the Shopify top products query, that's your conversion signal — not what Meta reports as attributed purchases. As always, Shopify is the source of truth.
