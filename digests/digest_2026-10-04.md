## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Scaling a winner: budget-limited vs delivery-limited, and how to raise spend without breaking it.**

*Builds on: Budget Utilisation Rate and Significant edits & CBO pauses.* Those lessons covered what spend vs budget tells you. This one covers what to do when you find an ad set that's earning.

- **Budget-limited** means the ad set spends its full budget every day and would happily spend more. The budget is the ceiling. Raising it buys more of the same customers, at a gradually worse price (diminishing returns).
- **Delivery-limited** means the ad set spends *less* than its budget. The ceiling is something else: the audience is small, the bid strategy won't pay more, or the creative has stopped winning auctions. Raising the budget here does **nothing**. The extra money just sits unused, or CBO gives it to a weaker ad set in the same campaign.
- **Vertical scaling** means raising the budget on the same ad set. Meta treats a large budget jump as a *significant edit* that can send the ad set back into learning, where performance is unstable for days. The operator's rule of thumb is **steps of about 20%, 2–3 days apart.**
- **Horizontal scaling** means adding new ad sets or creatives (a duplicate with a broader audience, or fresh ads in the same ad set). It's the route when an ad set is delivery-limited.

**Worked example (3 Oct):** NEW-HOT bought 6 purchases at **£4.08 CPP** (£6.12 over 3 days). Break-even CPA is **£17.13** (£28.60 AOV ÷ 1.67x), so it has roughly 4× of headroom on paper. But its campaign spent **£25.19 of £30 (84%)**, and NEW-HOT came in under £28 on **6 of the last 8 days**. **It is delivery-limited most days**, so a bigger budget would mostly go unspent, or leak to "New Sales ad set," which spent £0.73 and isn't winning. Suppose a +20% step (£30 → £36) did get spent and the extra £6 bought purchases at double the current CPP (£12.24, which assumes diminishing returns). That's 0.49 extra purchases, about £14 of revenue, a **2.3x marginal return** on that £6. That's worthwhile, but small. The bigger lever is horizontal: put NEW-HOT's winning creative into a duplicate with a wider audience.

**Ask Daryl this:** "NEW-HOT has spent under its £30 cap on 6 of the last 8 days. Is that the audience running out or the bid strategy holding it back, and is the plan to scale it by budget, a duplicate, or new creative?" A **good answer** names the cause (audience size or frequency rising, auction competition) and commits to a step and a date. An **evasive answer** is "the algorithm's optimising" or "it's doing well, leave it."

TikTok works the same way: large budget jumps can restart learning there too, so scale in moderate steps or duplicate the ad group.

**You should now be able to** tell from Windsor's spend vs budget whether a winning ad set can be scaled with budget, or whether it needs new ad sets and creative instead.
