## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Underspend: budget-limited vs bid-limited**

*Builds on Smart Bidding (Max Value vs tROAS).* A daily budget is a **ceiling**, not an order. What decides spend is the **bid**, meaning how much Google will pay for each auction. "Maximise Conversion Value" can carry an optional **target ROAS** (a minimum return per £). In the data it still shows as Max Conversion Value. With a target set, Google skips any auction it predicts won't pay back, so the campaign can sit well under budget for days.

There are three causes, and each looks different from the outside:
- **Bid ceiling (tROAS):** CPC ↓ and impressions ↓. Google is buying fewer, cheaper auctions.
- **Demand drop:** CPC flat, impressions ↓. Fewer people are searching.
- **Feed or policy fault:** spend falls off a cliff to near £0.

**Worked:** PMax spent £164.84 of £400 (41%). CPC was £0.63 vs a £0.78 average, and impressions were 26,402 vs 41,166. **Both are down, so this looks like a bid ceiling.**

**TikTok:** its Minimum ROAS and Cost Cap bidding underspend the same way.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Is there a target ROAS on PMax AUG, at what %, and since when?" A good answer gives a number and a date. An evasive one says "Google's optimising."

**You should now be able to** tell from 3 numbers why a campaign isn't spending its budget.
