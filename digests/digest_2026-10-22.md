## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Data-driven attribution: why Google reports 6.98 purchases**

*This builds on Cross-Platform Attribution Overlap.* That lesson covered credit fights *between* platforms. This one covers credit *inside* Google. A buyer often clicks two Google ads before buying, say a Shopping ad Monday and another campaign's ad Wednesday. **Data-driven attribution (DDA)** is Google's default rule for sharing out that one sale. It compares paths that converted with paths that didn't, and splits the credit, e.g. 0.6 / 0.4. That's why conversions come out as fractions. Paths keep being re-scored for days, so the newest days' numbers are provisional.

**Worked example:** on 14 Oct, ALL OTHER CSS was credited with 6.9838 conversions. Yesterday Google credited 8 (7 + 1, so £208.51 on £180.19 = 1.16x) and Facebook 12, against 25 Shopify orders. That's why the headline ROAS stays on Shopify.

**What to watch for:** a campaign whose credited conversions rise days later. Judge Google over 3+ days, never on yesterday alone. Claude flags restatements. TikTok defaults to last-touch inside its own window.

**You should now be able to** explain a fractional conversion, and why yesterday's Google number isn't final.
