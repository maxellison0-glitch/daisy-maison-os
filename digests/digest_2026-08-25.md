## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Google Ads Change History — the audit trail you are flying blind without

**Callback:** On 22 Aug we covered PMax campaign rebuilds — the new PMAX CSS AUG replaced PMAX CSS DEC, a structural change you spotted only because the spend numbers shifted. But spotting a change after it shows up in revenue data is too late to intervene. The Change History log is how you see changes *before* the revenue tells you something went wrong.

**What it is.** Every Google Ads account keeps a complete, timestamped, permanent log of every change made to the account. It records three things for each entry: **who** made the change (the person's email address, an automated rule, or the Google system itself), **when** they made it (date and time), and **what** they changed (the specific setting, the old value, and the new value). You find it in Google Ads under **Tools & Settings → Change history** in the left navigation panel. The log is filterable — narrow by date range, by campaign, by the type of change (budget, bid strategy, status, ad creation), and by who made it. An account like Daisy Maison has dozens of changes per week across multiple campaigns; reading the full log chronologically is noise. Filter to the campaign or setting you care about and read only that.

**Three types of actor show up in the log:**

1. **Named users** — real email addresses. Daryl's email, his team's, yours eventually. Every manual change they make gets a row: adjusting a budget, pausing a campaign, changing a bid strategy target, editing an ad headline. If Daryl changed the PMAX CSS AUG daily budget from £280 to £360 on Thursday, the log shows: *"daryl@..., 21 Aug 14:23, Campaign daily budget, PMAX CSS AUG, £280 → £360."*

2. **Automated rules** — scripts or rules set up inside the account. If someone built a rule that says "increase budget by 10% when ROAS exceeds 3.0x," every time that rule fires, the log records it: *"Automated rule: Scale winners, 22 Aug 09:00, Campaign daily budget, PMAX CSS AUG, £280 → £308."* This is important because an automated rule can be why spend keeps creeping above a recommendation — no human is making the change.

3. **System changes** — Google itself. Smart Bidding micro-adjustments at each auction are too frequent to log, but structural system actions do appear: Google auto-applying a recommendation (it can do this if auto-apply is turned on), or the system adjusting a target ROAS. These are labelled "Google Ads system" and they are the most dangerous because nobody asked for them.

**What the log does NOT cover:** Facebook/Meta changes (Meta has its own Activity Log — hamburger menu in Ads Manager → Activity Log, or the History tab on any campaign row). Merchant Center changes (product approvals, feed errors — those live in merchant.google.com under Products → Diagnostics). And it does not record performance data. It records *actions*, not *results*.

**Worked example with yesterday's numbers.** PMAX CSS AUG spent **£359.48** on 24 Aug. The recommendation is £280/day. Two scenarios:

*Scenario A:* Daryl set the daily budget at £280. Google allows up to **200% of the daily budget on any single day** (the 30-day monthly average must stay at or below budget × 30.4). At £280, Google can spend up to £560 on one day. Yesterday's £359 is within that window. The Change History would show *"Budget set: £280"* with no subsequent change — the overshoot is algorithmic and self-correcting over the month.

*Scenario B:* The budget was never set at £280 — it was created at £360 or higher. The Change History would show *"Campaign created, daily budget £360"* with no reduction to £280. Every day's spend has been at or near the intended level, the "overshoot" is the budget working as set, and the recommendation was ignored.

The arithmetic: over 4 days (21–24 Aug), PMAX CSS AUG has spent £226 + £231 + £330 + £359 = **£1,146**. At £280/day for 4 days, the budget would total £1,120 — close enough that Scenario A is plausible. But at £360/day, the total would be up to £1,440 and the algorithm would actually be running *below* budget. You cannot distinguish these without reading the log. The revenue consequence: at yesterday's 2.05x blended ROAS, every extra £80/day above £280 produced ~£164 in total sales, roughly £98 gross profit against £80 spend = £18 net contribution. Barely positive at the *blended* rate, and the marginal rate is always worse (diminishing returns, 28 Jul lesson).

**Facebook equivalent:** Meta Ads Manager → hamburger (≡) → **Activity Log**, or any campaign row → hover → **See history**. Same data: who, when, what changed, old value → new value. For TEST OCT 2022's 4-day absence, the Activity Log would show either a manual pause (with the email) or a budget-to-zero event, answering in seconds what 4 days of asking have not.

**TikTok Ads Manager** has the same feature under Account Settings → Change History, with a similar filter-by-campaign interface. Same diagnostic applies.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Can you send me a screenshot of the Change History for PMAX CSS AUG, filtered to the last 14 days? I want to see when it was created, what the daily budget was set to, and whether any automated rules are running on it. Separately, a screenshot of the Activity Log for TEST OCT 2022 showing the last event — I want to know whether it was paused and by whom." A good answer is two timestamped screenshots showing the creation event, budget settings, and the pause event. An evasive answer is "it's all handled" without the screenshots — the Change History is the basic operational record of the account, and sharing it takes 30 seconds.

**You should now be able to** open the Change History in a Google Ads account (or Activity Log in Facebook Ads Manager), filter to a specific campaign and date range, and determine whether a budget recommendation was actually implemented or ignored — the difference between algorithmic behaviour you can trust and human inaction you need to fix.
