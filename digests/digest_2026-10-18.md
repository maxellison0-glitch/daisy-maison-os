## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Seasonality adjustments: warning Google before a promotion**

*This builds on Smart Bidding (Maximise Conversion Value vs target ROAS).* Smart Bidding sets each bid from the **conversion rate** it predicts (the share of clicks that become orders), and it learns that rate from the last few weeks. A short promotion, like a free-delivery weekend or Black Friday, changes the real rate faster than the model notices. So it underbids during the spike and then overbids for days after the sale ends. A **seasonality adjustment** (Tools → Bid strategies → Advanced controls) tells Google: "for these dates, expect conversion rate +X%". It's for 1–7 day events only, and the bidding goes back to normal afterwards without unlearning anything. The way it fails: you guess X too high and pay too much for every click. Meta has no equivalent, and neither does TikTok. Both just react to the pixel.

**Worked example (17 Oct):** CVR was 3.49% vs 3.74% over the last 7 days, so the free-delivery offer didn't lift it. A +30% adjustment would have bid for 3.74 × 1.3 = 4.86%, so every click would have been overpriced by 4.86 ÷ 3.49 = 1.39×.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Will you set a seasonality adjustment for Black Friday, and what % will you use?" Good answer: a % worked out from last year's Black Friday CVR vs normal days. Evasive answer: "Smart Bidding handles it."

**You should now be able to** tell when a promo deserves a seasonality adjustment, and when one would just waste money.
