## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Incrementality: would they have bought anyway?**

*This builds on new-customer CPA.* nCAC strips out returning buyers. Incrementality goes a step further and asks how many of the sales an ad claims it actually *caused*. **Incremental** means "wouldn't have happened without the ad." Platforms count every buyer who clicked or viewed, including people already on their way to you. The only real test is a **holdout**: a randomly chosen group is kept from seeing the ads, and you compare how much each group buys. Meta calls this Conversion Lift (Experiments). A cruder version is a geo or on/off test. The failure looks like this from outside: you cut a campaign, its platform-reported sales vanish, and Shopify barely moves.

**Worked example (23 Oct):** Facebook claimed 13 purchases on £151.67, so CPP £11.67. Suppose a holdout showed 30% would have bought anyway (an assumption, not measured). That leaves 9.1 real purchases, and the true CPP is **£16.67**, 43% worse.

**What to watch for:** natural experiments. Christmas Static fell from £23/day to £2–7 on 22–23 Oct, and Shopify sales *rose* (£944.51, £1,061.59). Claude tracks whether that holds. TikTok offers lift studies too, through a rep.

**You should now be able to** tell a campaign that creates sales from one that collects credit for them.
