## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Ad relevance diagnostics: Meta's three report-card rankings**

*This builds on "Taking CPP apart" (CPM, CTR and click-to-purchase rate).* Meta grades every ad that has 500+ impressions against other ads competing for the same audience. It gives three rankings: **Quality** (does it look like a good ad, or like clickbait/low effort), **Engagement** (are people likely to tap, comment or click), and **Conversion rate** (are the people who click likely to buy). Each one comes back as Above average, Average, or Below average (bottom 35%, 20% or 10%). They live in Ads Manager → Columns → Customise → "Quality ranking", "Engagement rate ranking" and "Conversion rate ranking". The point is that each one points at a different fix. Low quality or engagement: change the creative. Low conversion with high engagement: the ad is promising something the landing page doesn't deliver.

**Worked example (15 Oct):** test_xmas_creatives got 224 clicks ÷ 7,664 impressions = **2.92% CTR**, the best in the account. But 2 purchases ÷ 224 clicks = **0.89%** of clickers bought. test_creatives_2 converted 5 ÷ 118 = **4.24%**, and your Reel 3 ÷ 41 = **7.3%**. Christmas has engagement and loses at conversion. That's the pattern where the ad looks good but the landing page doesn't deliver.

**Ask Daryl this:** "What conversion rate ranking do the test_xmas_creatives ads show, and does the ad match the page it lands on?" Good answer: he names the ranking and the landing page. Evasive answer: "CTR is strong, so it's working."

**TikTok:** there's no equivalent ranking, so you work out CTR and click-to-purchase yourself.

**You should now be able to** open three columns and tell whether a weak ad needs new creative or a better landing page.
