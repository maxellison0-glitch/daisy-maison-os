## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Facebook's Daily Budget: The 25% Overspend Rule & Where the Spend Lever Actually Lives

**Builds on:** Budget scaling rules (23 Jun) — we learned *when* to increase budgets (3–5 green days, then +20% per change). Today we learn *how* budgets actually work inside Facebook once they're set, because yesterday's data showed the gap between agreeing a spend level and controlling it.

---

When you say "hold TOF-WEDDING at £125/day," that instruction only works if the actual daily budget inside Facebook Ads Manager is set to £125. Yesterday, TOF-WEDDING spent £226.94 — 84% above the hold level. Either the budget was changed, or the budget was never set where you think it was. Understanding the mechanics is how you tell the difference.

**What a "daily budget" actually means on Facebook**

A daily budget on Facebook is not a hard cap. It is a *target average*. Facebook explicitly reserves the right to spend **up to 25% more than your daily budget on any individual day**, as long as the total spend across the week doesn't exceed the daily budget multiplied by seven. So a £125 daily budget can legally spend up to £156.25 on a busy day (£125 × 1.25), as long as it underspends on quieter days to compensate.

This is by design, not a bug. Facebook's auction runs in real time, and when it spots a cluster of high-probability buyers — say, on a Saturday evening when gift-buying intent peaks — it wants the flexibility to chase them even if it means exceeding the daily average. The trade-off is that you give up per-day precision in exchange for the algorithm capturing better opportunities across the week.

**Yesterday's TOF-WEDDING was not a 25% overspend day.** At £227 on a £125 budget, that's 81.5% over — well beyond the 25% buffer. The 25% rule maxes out at £156.25. So one of two things happened: either the daily budget was raised above £125 (perhaps to £200 or higher), or the campaign uses a structure called **Campaign Budget Optimisation (CBO)** where the budget is set at the campaign level and Facebook distributes it across ad sets automatically.

**CBO vs ABO: where the lever lives**

There are two ways to set budgets on Facebook:

1. **Ad Set Budget Optimisation (ABO)** — you set a separate daily budget on each ad set within a campaign. You control exactly how much each audience segment gets. This is the old-school approach and gives the most granular control.

2. **Campaign Budget Optimisation (CBO)** — you set ONE daily budget for the entire campaign, and Facebook's algorithm decides how to split it across ad sets based on real-time performance. This is Meta's default for new campaigns and their recommended approach.

Under CBO, you cannot directly control how much each ad set spends. If Facebook thinks one ad set's audience is responding better, it will shift budget there — even if that means one ad set gets 80% of the campaign budget and another gets 5%. This is powerful when the algorithm is right and dangerous when it's wrong, because you lose visibility into where your money actually went.

**The worked example: yesterday's numbers**

Yesterday, the Daisy Maison Facebook account ran three campaigns totalling £407.61:

- TOF-WEDDING: **£226.94** (891 clicks at £0.25 CPC)
- EOI-Multiple: **£96.83** (507 clicks at £0.19 CPC)
- TEST OCT 2022: **£83.84** (173 clicks at £0.48 CPC)

On 3 Aug (the last green day), the same account spent £205.35 total: TOF-WEDDING £123.01, TEST OCT 2022 £82.34, and no EOI-Multiple at all.

The difference: £202.26 more Facebook spend, almost entirely from TOF-WEDDING's extra £104 and EOI-Multiple's £97. The store produced virtually identical revenue both days (£1,811 vs £1,992 — and £1,992 was on a Monday, typically a stronger day than Sunday). The extra £202 bought 1,571 clicks instead of 665 — more than double the traffic — but the conversion rate fell from 6.17% to 4.07%. Cheap, high-volume clicks don't convert like search-intent clicks.

To diagnose this, you need to know: what is the daily budget set at for TOF-WEDDING? Is the campaign running CBO or ABO? Was the budget changed between 3 Aug and 9 Aug? Was EOI-Multiple deliberately reactivated, or did it turn back on through an automated rule?

**Ask Daryl this:** "What is the daily budget set at for TOF-WEDDING right now — the actual number in Ads Manager — and is the campaign running CBO or ABO? And did you reactivate EOI-Multiple, or did it come back on its own?" A good answer gives you the exact budget number and tells you the structure. An evasive answer says "I'm optimising it" without specifics — and that's the answer that tells you the most, because it means he doesn't want you to see the control.

**You should now be able to:** look at any day's Facebook spend, compare it to the agreed daily budget, and know whether the overspend is within Facebook's 25% buffer (normal, ignore it) or evidence that the budget itself was changed (a decision someone made without telling you).
