## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) — the number ROAS doesn't tell you**

You already know ROAS: for every £1 you spend on ads, how many £ of revenue come back. Yesterday's ROAS was 2.62x, which feels like a simple score. But ROAS has a blind spot — it tells you nothing about *how many customers* you're buying or what each one actually costs to acquire.

That's where CPA (Cost Per Acquisition, also called Cost Per Order) comes in.

**The formula is simple:**
CPA = total ad spend ÷ total orders

Yesterday: £581.21 ÷ 56 orders = **£10.38 per order.**

That means for every customer who placed an order yesterday, you spent an average of £10.38 in ads to get them there.

**Why does this matter when you already have ROAS?**

Because ROAS and CPA answer different questions:

- ROAS = *efficiency* — am I getting enough revenue back per pound spent?
- CPA = *cost of growth* — how much does each new customer cost me?

They're related, but not the same. Here's the bridge: if your AOV is £27.19 and your CPA is £10.38, your ad spend is eating **38%** of each order's revenue before you've counted product cost, Shopify fees, shipping, or your own time. If your gross margin on a wedding sign is roughly 60–65%, you're netting around £16–18 in gross profit per order — and then paying £10.38 for the customer. That leaves about **£6–7 gross contribution per ad-attributed order** after ad costs. Not nothing, but not comfortable either.

Now flip it: if CPA dropped to £7 (by improving CVR, or by the campaign maturing out of its learning phase and finding better buyers), that £6–7 contribution per order becomes £9–11. Same ad budget. Same products. Meaningfully better economics.

**Where this gets practical for you:**

Different campaigns have wildly different CPAs even if you can't see it at campaign level in Shopify. [RDD] TOF - WEDDING is getting 328 clicks at £0.37 and Mr & Mrs is the #1 product with 18 orders. If that campaign is driving even 10 of those orders, its CPA is roughly £12.28 (£122.80 ÷ 10). Not bad at all for a wedding gift product with a £27 AOV.

The [RDD] Teacher Gifts campaign spent £42.42 for 143 clicks and appeared to drive zero visible teacher orders. Its current CPA is effectively infinite — you're spending, nothing is converting yet. This doesn't mean kill it (you know from the cold-campaign-runway lesson that ~50 conversions are needed before the algorithm finds its footing), but it does explain why total ROAS is being dragged below 3x right now. You're subsidising the teacher campaign's learning phase with money that isn't coming back yet.

**The practical CPA benchmark for Daisy Maison:**

If your average gross margin is ~60%, break-even CPA on a £27 AOV order = 60% × £27 = **£16.20**. At £10.38, you're spending less than the break-even, which means the ads are still profitable on a gross contribution basis even at 2.62x ROAS. The goal of 3x ROAS corresponds to a CPA of about £9 at the current AOV. You're not far off — tightening the teacher budget by £20–30/day while it's in learning would push CPA down and nudge ROAS closer to target without abandoning the campaign.

This is the tool for deciding when a campaign is "too expensive to run" — not by ROAS feel, but by: *is my CPA below my gross margin on a typical order?* If yes, keep it running. If no, something needs to change.
