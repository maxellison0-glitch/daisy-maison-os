## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Account Structure: Why Fewer Ad Sets Beat More Ad Sets

**Callback:** Yesterday you learned about Learning Limited — the delivery status that means an ad set can't reach ~50 conversion events per week and gets stuck with a degraded targeting model. Today's lesson explains why that happens more often than it should, and how the number of ad sets directly controls it.

**The concept in plain English**

Facebook's algorithm needs data to learn who is most likely to buy from your store. Each ad set maintains its own independent targeting model — a statistical profile of your ideal buyer built from the Purchase events that specific ad set generated. The critical threshold is approximately 50 purchase events per week per ad set. Below that, the model is unstable and Facebook delivers your ads to a wider, less qualified audience (Learning Limited). Above it, the model stabilises and gets progressively better at finding buyers.

Here is the mechanism: **the total conversion volume of your store is fixed on any given day.** Yesterday Daisy Maison had 42 orders. Facebook attributed 19 of those to its ads. Those 19 purchases are the entire daily contribution — the raw material that 11 ad sets must share to train their individual targeting models.

**The arithmetic is unforgiving.** If you run 11 ad sets and generate ~19 FB-attributed purchases per day, that is roughly 133 per week. For every ad set to reach 50 events/week, you would need 11 × 50 = 550 weekly purchases from Facebook — nearly 4× what you actually produce. The math does not close. Most ad sets will be permanently Learning Limited, permanently delivering ads to the wrong people, permanently wasting budget.

**Why adding campaigns makes it worse**

Three days ago the account had 3 Facebook campaigns and about 8 ad sets. Today there are 5 campaigns and 11 ad sets. The new campaigns (Christmas Static Posts on 4 Sep, TOF - Diffusers on 6 Sep) added 4 ad sets. Each one now claims a slice of the 19 daily purchases. The new ad sets start at zero purchase data and need weeks to accumulate enough events — meanwhile they spend real money learning.

This is the fragmentation problem. It is not about whether each individual campaign has a good reason to exist — they do. Christmas testing is smart. Diffuser products need their own targeting. The problem is whether the store's total conversion volume can support the number of independent targeting models running simultaneously. At 42 orders/day, it cannot support 11.

**The consolidation principle**

The optimal number of ad sets is: **total weekly Facebook-attributed purchases ÷ 50**. For Daisy Maison right now: 133 ÷ 50 ≈ 2.7. Round up to 3. The account could sustain about 3 ad sets with healthy learning. It is running 11.

This does not mean you can only have 3 ads. An ad set can contain multiple ads (creatives). The ad set is the audience and budget container; the ads inside it are the creative variations. You can test 5 different creatives inside a single ad set without fragmenting the conversion signal. The distinction agencies often blur: creative testing belongs inside ad sets, not across separate ad sets with separate budgets.

**Worked example from yesterday**

TOF-WEDDING has 4 ad sets: TOF - MAIN (7 purchases), test_creatives (5), more (3), test_creatives_2 (0). That is 15 purchases across 4 ad sets = roughly 105/week total. Only TOF - MAIN (~49/week) is close to the 50-event threshold. test_creatives_2 at 0 purchases is a dead ad set consuming £13.57/day.

If TOF-WEDDING consolidated from 4 to 2 ad sets (merge test_creatives into MAIN, test_creatives_2 into more), each would accumulate ~52–53 purchases/week — both above the learning threshold, both optimising properly.

Similarly, Social Engagement runs NEW - HOT (2 purchases) and New Sales (0). Combined: 2/day = ~14/week — still below 50, but 14 in one ad set learns faster than 7 in each of two.

**The timing trap**

The worst time to add new campaigns is when total conversion volume is falling. Yesterday's 42 orders is 24% below the 30 Aug Sunday (55 orders). Each new ad set is fighting over a shrinking pie. A new campaign launched during a volume upswing (the 82-order day on 14 Aug) has a much better chance of accumulating 50 events quickly because the pie is larger.

**TikTok note:** TikTok's equivalent threshold is lower (~20–30 conversion events per week per ad group) because its audience signals refresh faster with shorter content cycles, but the fragmentation principle is identical — more ad groups on the same volume means each one learns slower.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Can you pull up the Delivery column at ad set level for all 11 active ad sets and tell me how many show 'Active' versus 'Learning' versus 'Learning Limited'? I want to see which ad sets have enough conversion volume to actually optimise." A good answer names each ad set's status specifically: "TOF - MAIN is Active, test_creatives is Learning, these four are Learning Limited." An evasive answer: "They're all performing fine" without showing the Delivery column.

**You should now be able to:** look at the number of active ad sets, compare it to weekly purchase volume, and immediately judge whether the account is structurally fragmented or correctly sized — before looking at any performance metric.
