## Lesson of the Day

### Learning Limited — The Most Important Status You Will See in Ads Manager

**CALLBACK:** On 6 Jun you learned what the learning phase is — the ~50-purchase-event window a new ad set needs before its algorithm stabilises. On 5 Sep you learned how Min ROAS bidding throttles delivery to protect profitability. Today's lesson sits at the intersection: what happens when throttled delivery prevents the algorithm from collecting enough data to learn properly, and why the delivery status column is the single most important thing to check every morning when you sit down at the console.

**What "Learning Limited" means in plain English.** Every ad set in Facebook Ads Manager has a "Delivery" column. It shows one of several statuses: Active, Learning, Learning Limited, Not Delivering, In Review, or Error. "Learning" means the ad set is still inside its initial ~50-purchase-event window and the algorithm is experimenting with who to show ads to. "Active" means it has exited learning and is running on stable, optimised targeting. "Learning Limited" means the ad set TRIED to exit learning but could not gather enough conversion events — roughly 50 purchase events per week — and is now stuck in a degraded state where the algorithm never fully stabilises. It still runs. It still spends. But its targeting decisions are noisier and less efficient than an ad set that reached "Active."

**The mechanism — why it happens and why it matters.** Facebook's algorithm learns by pattern-matching: it shows your ad to people, sees who buys, builds a statistical profile of buyers, and then shows the ad to more people who match that profile. Each purchase event refines the profile. Below about 50 events per week, the profile is too thin — the algorithm cannot distinguish between genuine buyer signals and random noise. It keeps bidding, but its bids are based on a weak model rather than a strong one, which means higher CPP, lower ROAS, and more volatility day to day.

The critical point: Learning Limited is not the same as failing. A Learning Limited ad set can still produce profitable conversions. But it will never be as efficient as one that reached Active, because its targeting model never stabilises. Every day it runs in this state, it is spending money less efficiently than it could be.

**The three common causes — and what to do about each.**

1. **Budget too low.** The ad set does not have enough daily budget to generate ~7 purchases per day (50 ÷ 7 days). If your CPP is £12 and your daily budget is £50, you can afford ~4 purchases per day — below the threshold. Fix: raise the budget, or consolidate ad sets so the budget is shared across fewer, higher-volume ad sets.

2. **Audience too narrow.** The target audience is too small for the algorithm to find enough buyers. This is rarer on broad targeting but common on custom audiences or interest stacks. Fix: broaden the audience, or switch to Advantage+ Audience (Facebook's automatic broadening).

3. **Conversion event too rare.** This is the trap for stores with lower order volume. If the store does 47 orders per day across ALL traffic sources, and an ad set captures 3–4 of those purchases, it will never reach 50/week. Fix: you can switch the optimisation event from Purchase to Add to Cart (more frequent), but this degrades the model because cart-adds are a weaker buying signal than purchases. The real fix is consolidation — fewer ad sets, each with a larger share of the conversion volume.

**Why this matters right now for CHRISTMAS STATIC POSTS.** Yesterday's data: £16.43 spent across two ad sets, 4 purchases total. That is strong on day 2. But split across two ad sets, the volume is approximately 2 purchases per ad set per day — roughly 14 per week if sustained. That is well below the 50-event threshold. Both ad sets are almost certainly showing "Learning" right now, and if the volume does not increase, they will transition to "Learning Limited" within 7–10 days.

This is not necessarily a problem if the ROAS stays strong. But it does mean the algorithm will never fully optimise its targeting for these ad sets. Every auction bid will be based on incomplete data. At £16/day this is tolerable — the Min ROAS floor prevents unprofitable bids regardless of learning status. But if Daryl plans to scale this campaign to £100+/day for the Christmas season, he needs to get at least one ad set to Active first, which means consolidating the two test ad sets into one (combining their conversion volume) or raising the budget enough that a single ad set generates 50 purchases per week.

**Worked example using yesterday's numbers.** CHRISTMAS STATIC POSTS has two ad sets: test_xmas_creatives (£3.84 spend, 1 purchase) and test_xmas_creatives2 (£12.59 spend, 3 purchases). At a combined 4 purchases per day, the campaign as a whole would hit 28 per week — above the threshold if it were ONE ad set, but below if split. The fix is to merge the two test ad sets into one once you have enough data to pick the winning creative (which requires ~15–20 purchases per ad set — about 5–7 more days at current rates). Until then, both run in Learning, and the algorithm cannot fully optimise either.

Contrast this with TOF - MAIN (the largest TOF-WEDDING ad set): £102.47 spend, 8 purchases yesterday. At 8 per day, that is ~56 per week — above the 50-event threshold. TOF - MAIN is almost certainly in "Active" status, which is why its targeting is stable and its CPP (£12.81) is consistent day to day. The three smaller ad sets (test_creatives at 3, more at 3, test_creatives_2 at 1) are all likely Learning Limited or still in Learning, which is why their CPP is more volatile.

**Where to check.** In Ads Manager, switch to the ad set view (not campaign view). The Delivery column is visible by default. If it shows "Learning" with a progress indicator, the ad set is still collecting data. If it shows "Learning Limited," the ad set has given up trying to reach 50 events and is running on an incomplete model. If it shows "Active," the model is stable. Check this column FIRST, before looking at CPP or ROAS — it tells you whether the numbers you are reading are reliable or noisy.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Which ad sets across all campaigns are currently showing 'Learning Limited' in the Delivery column? If any TOF-WEDDING ad set is Learning Limited, it should either be consolidated into TOF - MAIN or given a higher budget to cross the 50-event threshold. For CHRISTMAS STATIC POSTS, is the plan to consolidate the two test ad sets into one before scaling for Christmas?" A good answer names the specific statuses and has a consolidation plan. An evasive answer is "they are all running fine" without checking the Delivery column — "running" and "running efficiently" are different things.

**You should now be able to:** open Ads Manager, read the Delivery column for every ad set, identify which ones are Learning Limited, calculate whether their daily budget and CPP can produce 50 events per week, and decide whether to consolidate, raise budget, or accept the efficiency cost of a small ad set.
