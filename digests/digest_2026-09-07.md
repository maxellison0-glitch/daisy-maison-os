## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Frequency Capping — Why Your Best Ad Becomes Your Worst Ad

**Callback:** On 18 Aug we learned about creative fatigue vs audience exhaustion — that declining CTR with flat CPM means the creative has gone stale, while rising CPM with stable CTR means the audience is spent. Today's lesson is the MECHANISM behind creative fatigue: frequency. Understanding frequency gives you a number to watch so you can diagnose fatigue before CTR collapses, not after.

**What frequency is.** Frequency is the average number of times one person in your audience has seen your ad over a given period. Facebook reports it at the ad set level in Ads Manager. If your ad set reached 10,000 people and served 30,000 impressions, your frequency is 3.0 — each person saw the ad an average of three times.

This sounds harmless. Three times is nothing — you barely notice a billboard the first time you drive past it. But Facebook is not a billboard. A billboard sits on a road you chose to drive down. A Facebook ad is injected into a feed you are scrolling for personal reasons. The tolerance for repetition is dramatically lower because the person never asked to see it.

**Why frequency kills performance.** The mechanism works in three stages:

Stage 1 (frequency 1.0–1.5): The ad is new to most of the audience. People who are interested click. CTR is at its natural peak and CPC is at its natural floor. This is the honeymoon period.

Stage 2 (frequency 2.0–3.0): Most of the audience has now seen the ad at least twice. The people who were going to click have already clicked. The people who were not interested the first time are not interested the second time either — but Facebook keeps showing them the ad because there is nobody else in the audience definition left to show it to. CTR starts falling because the denominator (impressions) keeps rising while the numerator (clicks) does not. CPC rises because Facebook's auction model charges more when your ad is less engaging (remember the 15 Jun lesson on ad quality score — estimated action rate is dropping, so your bid has to compensate). CPM may rise too, because Facebook is now competing harder in auctions to serve impressions to the shrinking pool of people who have not yet been overexposed.

Stage 3 (frequency 4.0+): The ad is now actively annoying. Some people hide it. Negative feedback signals reach Facebook's algorithm, which further depresses your ad quality score. You are now paying more per impression to reach people who are less likely to act. The ad that was once your best performer is now your worst — not because the creative changed, but because the audience's patience ran out.

**The worked example — yesterday's numbers.** TOF-WEDDING has been running for 29 consecutive days on the same creative set. At £189.96/day spend and £199/day budget, the campaign is nearly maxing its daily allocation — meaning Facebook is serving every impression it can find. The CPC has held at £0.31 for weeks, which looks stable, but CTR has slid from 1.55% (5 Sep) to 1.43% (yesterday) while spend stayed the same. That gap is frequency at work: the same people are seeing the same ads again, and fewer are clicking.

If the average frequency across TOF-WEDDING's ad sets is 3.5 over the last 7 days, and the audience size is 500,000, Facebook has delivered roughly 1.75 million impressions to those 500,000 people in a week — nearly 4 impressions per person. The people in that audience who were going to buy a personalised wedding sign have already either bought or decided not to. The remaining 490,000+ people have seen the ad multiple times and are not clicking.

Here is the arithmetic that makes this real. At frequency 1.5 (early in the campaign), a 1.55% CTR on, say, 50,000 daily impressions produces 775 clicks. At frequency 3.5 and 1.43% CTR on the same impressions, you get 715 clicks — 60 fewer potential customers per day on the same spend. Over a month, that is 1,800 fewer clicks for free. The CPC holds flat because Facebook adjusts impression volume, but the QUALITY of those clicks deteriorates because you are re-serving to an increasingly saturated pool.

**What you can do about it.** The controls are:

1. **Read the frequency column.** In Ads Manager → Columns → Customize Columns → search "Frequency" and add it. Read it at the ad set level over 7-day and 14-day windows. Below 2.0 over 7 days is healthy. Above 3.0 over 7 days means the audience is being overexposed.

2. **Expand the audience before adding creative.** A bigger audience pool means the same budget reaches fresh people. This is Broad targeting (removing interest and lookalike restrictions so Facebook's algorithm finds buyers across the whole platform). Many agencies resist this because it feels uncontrolled, but on a Purchase-optimised campaign, the algorithm's buyer model is more precise than most manual targeting anyway.

3. **Rotate creative.** Add new ads INTO the existing ad set (never edit or replace — editing resets the learning phase, as covered on 4 Jul). Three to five active ads per ad set gives Facebook material to rotate before any single ad fatigues. The algorithm will naturally shift spend to the fresher creative.

4. **Use frequency caps.** Under ad set → Budget & Schedule, Facebook allows you to set a frequency cap on Reach-optimised campaigns (not all objectives support it). For Purchase-optimised campaigns, the lever is audience size and creative variety instead.

**The TikTok difference.** TikTok audiences fatigue at roughly half the speed — frequency 2.0+ over 7 days is the danger zone on TikTok (vs 3.0+ on Facebook), because TikTok's full-screen format makes repetition more noticeable and the swipe-away threshold is lower. When Daisy Maison eventually runs TikTok ads, creative rotation needs to happen roughly twice as fast.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "What is the 7-day frequency on each of the TOF-WEDDING ad sets right now, and when was the last time new creative was added to any of them?" A good answer is a specific number per ad set and a date. An evasive answer is "the algorithm manages it" or "frequency is fine" without numbers — that means he has not checked, and the 29-day-old creative is probably running on momentum rather than performance.

**You should now be able to** open the TOF-WEDDING campaign in Ads Manager, add the Frequency column, read the 7-day number per ad set, and judge whether the declining CTR is a creative fatigue problem that needs fresh ads or an audience exhaustion problem that needs broader targeting.
