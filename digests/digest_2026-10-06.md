## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Google Smart Bidding: Maximise Conversion Value vs Target ROAS, and why loosening the target makes every click dearer.**

*Builds on: Minimum ROAS bidding on Facebook (21 Sep) and Google's daily budget is an average (30 Sep).* Facebook's Min ROAS is a floor that holds spend back. Google has the same lever, under a different name and with a different failure.

**The terms.** *Smart Bidding* is Google setting a separate bid in every auction, using signals you can't see: device, time, location, search history, the products being browsed. You choose the *goal*, and Google picks the price. For a shop, the two goals that matter are:
- **Maximise Conversion Value.** "Spend the whole budget and get as much sales value as possible." There's no efficiency guardrail, so the **budget is the only brake.**
- **Maximise Conversion Value with a Target ROAS (tROAS).** "Get as much value as possible, *but aim to return £X for every £1*." Google writes it as a percentage: 300% = 3.0x. Here the **target is the brake**, and the budget is a ceiling.

**The mechanism.** Before each auction, Google predicts two numbers: how likely this click is to convert, and the basket value if it does. Multiplied together, that's the *expected value of the click*. Under tROAS, the most it will bid is roughly **expected value ÷ target.** If a click is worth £1.20 in expectation:
- At 300% tROAS, Google will pay up to **£0.40.**
- At 200% it will pay up to **£0.60.**
- At 150% it will pay up to **£0.80.**

Lowering the target doesn't buy "more of the same clicks". It lets Google **win auctions it was losing, by paying more for each one.** From the outside it looks like this:
1. CPC up.
2. CPM up.
3. Spend up, day after day, until the budget caps it.
4. Clicks up by less than spend.
5. Revenue up, but by less than the spend.

That last step is the trap. Revenue *does* rise, so it looks as though it's working, while the marginal pound is coming back below break-even. The reverse failure is a target set too high. Then Google underspends its budget and volume dries up, which is what Min ROAS does on Facebook on a thin day.

Two more mechanics matter at the controls:
- **tROAS is measured on Google's own conversion value**, with its 30-day click attribution window. Google's in-platform ROAS will read higher than your Shopify ROAS. Set the target from your Shopify break-even plus a cushion, not from what Google reports.
- **Every target change resets learning.** Practitioners move it in steps of 10–20% and wait 1–2 weeks between moves.

**Worked example (5 Oct, real numbers).** The store took £1,473.72 from 930 sessions, so **one session was worth £1.58** in sales. That's store-wide, not Google-only. Treat it as an estimate. PMax paid **£0.96 a click** (7-day avg £0.77):
- At £0.77, a £1.58 visit returns **2.05x.**
- At £0.96 it returns **1.65x**, under the 1.67x break-even.

The £0.19 rise in CPC took a typical Google click from profitable to loss-making on its own. Across the account, the extra £227 of spend above average brought in £315 of sales (1.39x). That matches steps 1–5. **This is a hypothesis.** Windsor doesn't return Google's bid strategy or target, so the account itself has to confirm it.

**Where to look.** Google Ads → Campaigns → PMAX AUG → **Settings → Bidding**. Note the strategy and any target ROAS. Then check **Change history** for "Bid strategy" or "Target ROAS" entries since 29 Sep. **TikTok note:** TikTok's equivalent is *Value-Based Optimisation*, with "Highest value" (no brake) or "Minimum ROAS" (a floor). The same trade-off applies.

**Ask Daryl this:** "What is PMAX AUG's bid strategy and target ROAS today, what was it on 29 Sep, and what is the shared budget? Spend and CPC have risen four days running." **A good answer** gives two numbers and a reason, for example: "tROAS 350% → 250% to push Q4 volume, budget £300 → £450." **An evasive answer** is "the algorithm is scaling into Q4 demand", with no target named.

**You should now be able to** read a rising-CPC, rising-spend pattern as a loosened bid, put a pound value on what each click is worth, and know which two settings to check and reset.
