## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### When to Kill a Campaign — The Evaluation Framework

**Callback:** On 25 Jun you learned the "patience budget" — new campaigns look broken before they work, and you need a 7–14 day evaluation window before judging. On 20 Aug you learned the emergency stop protocol — when ROAS drops below break-even and the store loses money on every ad-attributed order. Today's lesson sits between those two: how to make the kill/keep decision once the evaluation window closes. This is the decision you just made on Wedding Diffuser, and understanding the framework means you can make it faster next time.

**Why this is the hardest ad decision.** Starting a campaign is psychologically easy — it is optimism, action, "let's test this." Killing one is painful because of sunk cost bias: you spent £229 on Wedding Diffuser, and killing it means accepting that money is gone with nothing to show for it. The temptation is always "one more day" because maybe it just needs more data. But every extra day costs £50–60 in spend, and the probability of a turnaround falls as data accumulates — more data confirming bad results is not ambiguity, it is evidence. The skill is knowing when "not enough data" becomes "enough data to say no."

**The Three-Gate Framework**

Every campaign evaluation passes through three gates. A campaign must clear all three to survive; failing any one is grounds to kill.

**Gate 1 — Time.** Has the campaign run long enough for Facebook's algorithm to exit learning phase? The minimum is 7 days or ~50 conversion events per ad set per week, whichever comes first. If neither threshold is met, the campaign is in its cold runway period and you cannot judge it fairly. But "not enough time" has an expiry date. If a campaign has run 7+ days and still has not generated anywhere near 50 conversions, it has failed Gate 1 by absence. The algorithm had its chance and could not find buyers at a viable cost.

**Gate 2 — Spend.** Has the campaign spent enough money for the results to be statistically meaningful? The rough rule: **3× your break-even CPA in total spend with zero purchases is a kill signal, not bad luck.** For Daisy Maison, break-even CPA is about £17.53 (typical AOV ~£29 × ~60% gross margin). So £52.59 in total spend with zero purchases means the campaign has bought enough auctions to find a buyer and has not. At £100+ with 0–1 purchases, the targeting or creative is not working — that is 6× your break-even threshold without a consistent result.

**Gate 3 — Cost Per Purchase (CPP).** If the campaign has generated purchases, is the blended CPP sustainable? Compare it to break-even CPA (£17.53):

- **Below 1.5× break-even (under ~£26):** Healthy. Scale cautiously (20% budget steps, per the 23 Jun lesson).
- **1.5–3× break-even (~£26–£53):** Marginal. Check trajectory — if CPP is falling day-over-day, the algorithm is learning. If flat or rising, it is stuck.
- **Above 3× break-even (over ~£53):** Burning money. Kill unless CPP is falling steeply and the campaign is under 7 days old.

The trajectory question is the tiebreaker: **is CPP falling day-over-day, or flat/rising?** Falling means the algorithm is finding cheaper buyers. Flat or rising means it is stuck with the audience or creative it has — more time will not fix it.

**Worked Example: Wedding Diffuser**

| Day | Spend | Purchases | CPP | Running blended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 Sep (day 1) | £60.22 | 1 | £60.22 | £60.22 |
| 6 Sep (day 2) | £46.19 | 0 | — | £106.41/1 = £106.41 |
| 7 Sep (day 3) | £60.22 | 1 | £60.22 | £166.63/2 = £83.32 |
| 8 Sep (day 4) | £61.24 | 1 | £61.24 | £227.87/3 = £75.96 |

Gate 1 — Time: 4 active days, below the 7-day minimum. Normally you would wait. But the spend threshold overrides...

Gate 2 — Spend: Total spend £227.87 = **13× break-even CPA** with only 3 purchases. The campaign has bought 13 break-even-CPAs' worth of auctions and found just 3 buyers.

Gate 3 — CPP: £75.96 blended = **4.3× break-even.** Trajectory: £60 → ∞ → £60 → £61. Flat. The algorithm is not learning to find cheaper buyers. Running another 3 days to reach the 7-day window would spend ~£170 more and yield perhaps 2 more purchases — ending at roughly £400 spent / 5 purchases / £80 CPP. Still deep in kill territory.

**Verdict:** Kill. The £229 is sunk cost; every additional day adds £50–60 to the loss. The diffuser sells organically (1.6 orders/day without ad support). If organic demand grows to 3+/day consistently, that is the signal to relaunch with new creative. Until then, it cannot sustain a standalone cold acquisition campaign.

**What "kill" means operationally:** Ads Manager → Wedding Diffuser → toggle the campaign OFF (not just the ad set — the entire campaign). Do not delete — pausing preserves pixel data and audience learnings for a potential relaunch. The campaign_status currently reads ACTIVE even though effective_status is PAUSED, meaning it could restart on its own or if someone unfreezes it.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "When you evaluate whether to keep or kill a campaign, what CPP threshold do you use — is it a fixed multiple of CPA, or a different rule? And do you look at trajectory or just the blended number?" A good answer names a specific number or formula and mentions trajectory (is it improving?). An evasive answer talks about "giving it more time" without naming a spend ceiling or a decision date — that is how £229 becomes £500 becomes £1,000.

**You should now be able to:** look at any campaign that has run 4+ days and, using its total spend, purchase count, blended CPP, and day-over-day CPP trajectory, make the kill/keep/hold decision within 60 seconds by running it through the three gates.
