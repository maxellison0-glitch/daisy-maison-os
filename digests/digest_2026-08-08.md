## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### The Ad Account as a Portfolio — Why Judging Each Campaign Solo Gives You the Wrong Answer

**Connects to:** Campaign objectives (29 Jun) taught you that each campaign type tells the algorithm which kind of person to find. Retargeting (21 Jun) showed you how the pixel builds warm audience pools. Today's lesson connects those pieces into a bigger picture: the ad account is a system where every campaign's output feeds another campaign's input, and you cannot optimise one piece in isolation.

---

Think of Daryl's ad account not as a collection of independent campaigns you can judge one by one, but as a **portfolio** — a set of investments that work together. Each campaign has a specific job in the pipeline, and evaluating any single campaign by its own direct ROAS is like evaluating a goalkeeper by goals scored. It measures the wrong thing for that player's role.

Here is how the portfolio works in your account, layer by layer:

**Layer 1 — Demand Capture: Google PMax CSS (£336, 468 clicks, £0.72 CPC).** When someone types "personalised wedding gift UK" into Google, your PMax campaign enters the Shopping auction and shows your Mr & Mrs Street Sign card with its price and photo. This is *demand capture* — the buyer already wants the thing and you are paying to appear when they search for it. CPC here is £0.72, the highest of any campaign you run. That is correct, not alarming. High-intent clicks are more valuable, so every advertiser bids harder for them and the auction price rises. A £0.72 click from someone who searched for exactly what you sell is worth far more than a £0.22 click from someone scrolling Facebook who has never heard of you.

**Layer 2 — Demand Creation: Facebook TOF-WEDDING (£128, 476 clicks, £0.27 CPC).** TOF stands for Top of Funnel. This campaign does not wait for people to search — it finds people who *look like* your existing buyers (using the lookalike audiences you learned about on 18 Jun) and puts your ads in their Facebook and Instagram feeds. Yesterday it sent 476 clicks to your site at just £0.27 each.

Here is the critical thing: most of those 476 people will not buy today. Maybe five or ten will. The rest will look at the product, perhaps add to cart, and leave. On a single-day spreadsheet, that looks like waste.

But it is not waste. Every one of those 476 visitors just got recorded by your Facebook Pixel. They have moved from "stranger" to "someone who visited the site and looked at a wedding gift." And that is where the next layer picks them up.

**Layer 3 — Remarketing: TEST OCT 2022 (£89, 167 clicks, £0.53 CPC).** This campaign has been running since October 2022. It targets people who have already visited your site and shows them the *specific products they viewed* — this is called dynamic remarketing. If someone looked at the Mr & Mrs Street Sign yesterday via a TOF-WEDDING click and left without buying, TEST OCT 2022 shows them that exact sign in their feed tomorrow, with the photo, the price, and a "shop now" button. Yesterday it sent 167 clicks at £0.53 CPC. Those 167 clicks are your warmest, most likely-to-buy traffic. The CPC is double TOF-WEDDING's because these warm visitors are more valuable and every advertiser competes harder for them.

**Layer 4 — Broad Discovery: EOI-Multiple (£48, 214 clicks, £0.22 CPC, 1.99% CTR).** This campaign lets Facebook decide which product to show each person based on what the algorithm thinks they will respond to. It is the cheapest CPC (£0.22) and highest CTR (1.99%) of any Facebook campaign because it casts the widest net. It operates alongside TOF-WEDDING to find people the lookalike model might miss.

**Why the system breaks if you optimise campaigns individually.** The mistake most store owners make when they start reading the numbers is to judge each campaign by direct orders and cut the ones that look expensive. "TOF-WEDDING costs £128 a day and only directly produces a handful of orders — cut it and save the money."

But if you cut TOF-WEDDING, here is what happens over the next five to seven days:
1. 476 fewer people visit your site each day.
2. Your Facebook Pixel pool shrinks — fewer new warm visitors feeding into it.
3. TEST OCT 2022's remarketing audience gets smaller and staler.
4. Within a week, TEST OCT 2022 starts showing ads to the same people repeatedly (frequency rises — you learned about this on 20 Jun).
5. Its CPC goes up, its conversion rate drops, and suddenly your "most profitable campaign" is broken.

You did not touch TEST OCT 2022. You cut the campaign that appeared to waste money. And the campaign you kept — the one that looked efficient — collapsed because you removed its fuel supply. This is why blended ROAS is the only honest metric.

**Worked example — yesterday's portfolio in action:**

| Layer | Campaign | Spend | Clicks | CPC | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand capture | Google PMax CSS | £403 | 599 | £0.67 | Catches people searching |
| Demand creation | FB TOF-WEDDING | £128 | 476 | £0.27 | Finds new prospects |
| Remarketing | FB TEST OCT 2022 | £89 | 167 | £0.53 | Converts warm visitors |
| Broad discovery | FB EOI-Multiple | £48 | 214 | £0.22 | Widens the net |
| **Total portfolio** | | **£668** | **1,456** | **£0.46** | **3.02x blended ROAS** |

Every one of yesterday's 73 orders probably touched more than one of these layers. A customer might have seen a TOF-WEDDING ad on Tuesday, searched Google on Thursday, and bought through a TEST OCT 2022 retargeting ad on Friday. All three campaigns would claim credit. That is why **Shopify total_sales ÷ total ad spend** is the only number that tells the truth. At £668 total spend with this campaign mix, the store generated £2,021 in revenue. That is the fact. Which campaign "caused" which sale is a philosophical question that no analytics platform can honestly answer.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "If we turned off TOF-WEDDING for one test week, what would happen to TEST OCT 2022's cost per purchase by the end of that week? And is EOI-Multiple pulling from the same warm audience pool or finding genuinely new people?" A good answer explains the dependency chain — how TOF feeds the remarketing pool and what happens when that feed dries up. An evasive answer is "it's all optimised, don't worry about it." That is not wrong, but it does not help you understand the system you are paying for.

**You should now be able to:** look at the campaign list in any morning digest, identify which layer of the portfolio each campaign serves (capture, creation, remarketing, or discovery), and explain to someone why cutting a cheap-looking TOF campaign would damage the expensive-looking remarketing campaign downstream — and why only the blended Shopify ROAS number matters.
