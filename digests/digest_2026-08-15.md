## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Google Merchant Center — The Product Feed That Powers Every PMax Click

**Callback:** When we covered Google Performance Max on 7 June, we described it as Google's automated campaign type that decides where, when, and to whom your products appear across Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube and Gmail. What we did not cover is where PMax gets its information about your products in the first place. That source is the Google Merchant Center — and it is the foundation the entire PMax system sits on. Every click you have ever seen from PMAX CSS DEC started life as a product listing inside this feed.

**What it is.** Google Merchant Center is a separate platform — merchant.google.com, not inside Google Ads — where your Shopify product catalogue lives as a "product feed." Every product in the store — its title, description, price, images, availability status, shipping information, and product attributes like colour, size, and material — gets exported from Shopify to Merchant Center automatically via the Google & YouTube sales channel app that Shopify installs. This feed is a structured data file, not a web page. It looks like a giant spreadsheet: one row per product variant, with columns for every attribute Google requires. When PMax enters an auction and decides whether to show one of your products to someone searching "personalised wedding gift," it does not use keywords the way old-style Google Search ads did. It reads the product feed. The title, description, and attributes in that feed ARE the targeting signal. This is why the title optimisation work in early July shifted which search queries your products appeared for within days — you were not changing a keyword bid, you were changing the raw text that Google uses to decide which queries match which products.

**How the sync works.** Shopify pushes your feed to Merchant Center on a regular schedule — typically every few hours, though you can trigger a manual sync from the Google & YouTube channel settings in Shopify admin. When you change a product title, update a price, add a new variant, or toggle a product from "Active" to "Draft," those changes propagate to Merchant Center on the next sync cycle. Merchant Center then runs every product through its validation rules. These rules are strict and non-negotiable:

- **Images** must be at least 100×100 pixels (250×250 for apparel), must show the actual product (not a logo, not a lifestyle shot without the product visible, not an image with promotional text overlaid like "SALE 50% OFF"), and must have a white or transparent background for the primary image. Secondary images can be lifestyle shots.
- **Prices** must exactly match what the landing page shows when Google's crawler visits it. If Merchant Center says £14.95 and the product page says £12.95 because you changed the price and the feed has not synced yet, Google will disapprove that product for "price mismatch."
- **Availability** must be accurate. If a product is marked "in stock" in the feed but shows "sold out" on the page, that is a policy violation.
- **Shipping and tax** information must be declared and must match what the customer actually pays at checkout.

Products that pass all validation are "approved" and enter the pool of products eligible to appear in PMax auctions. Products that fail are "disapproved" — they are pulled from every Shopping placement, every PMax auction, every time, until you fix the issue and the product re-validates.

**Where it goes wrong, and what the failure looks like from the outside.** The critical failure mode is that it is silent. A disapproved product does not show an error in Google Ads. PMax keeps running, keeps spending, keeps reporting clicks and impressions — just on fewer products than you think. If your top-selling product gets disapproved because someone updated the image and the new one accidentally has a promotional banner on it, PMax simply stops showing that product to anyone. It redistributes spend across your remaining approved products, your ROAS drops, and from the Google Ads console it looks like "PMax had a bad day" or "ROAS dipped for no reason." There is no alert in the Ads dashboard. The cause is visible only inside Merchant Center, in a completely different interface that many advertisers never check.

The second common failure is the price mismatch described above. Every price change on the live store — whether it is a product price, a variant price, or even a sale price being added or removed — creates a window where the feed and the landing page disagree. Google's crawler visits your pages on its own schedule (not instantly), and if it finds a mismatch, it disapproves the product until the prices align. On a store like Daisy Maison where add-on prices have been adjusted several times (easel £4.95→£3.95→£5.95, gift wrap £4.95→£5.95, strips £2.25→£1.99), each change created a brief mismatch risk. The fix is to trigger a manual feed sync in Shopify immediately after any price change, then check Merchant Center 30–60 minutes later to confirm the product is still approved.

**How to check it.** In Merchant Center, navigate to Products → Diagnostics. This page shows three numbers that tell you the feed's health in five seconds: total products submitted, total approved, and total disapproved, with the specific disapproval reasons listed below. The "Needs attention" tab shows products with warnings — not yet disapproved, but heading that way if not fixed. The number to watch is the **approval rate**: approved ÷ submitted. On a healthy feed, this should be above 95%. Below 90% means a meaningful share of your catalogue is invisible to every PMax auction, and your spend is concentrating on fewer products than intended.

**Worked example with yesterday's data.** PMAX CSS DEC spent **£342.75** yesterday and delivered **494 clicks** across **42,619 impressions**. That is the entire Google Shopping presence running through the CSS partner. But the question you cannot answer today: how many of Daisy Maison's products are actually approved and bidding in those auctions? If the store has, say, 150 active Shopify products but only 100 are approved in Merchant Center, then £342.75 of daily spend is being distributed across 100 products, not 150. The 50 disapproved products are generating zero impressions and zero revenue — but nothing in the Google Ads console tells you they are missing. The Mr & Mrs Street Sign at 31 orders is clearly approved and converting well. But is every wedding product, every street sign variant, every pebble picture actually in the auction? Is the Family Street Sign with its 4 orders yesterday getting a fair share of impressions, or is it being carried by three approved variants out of five? The Merchant Center diagnostics page answers all of this in 30 seconds.

To compute the practical cost: if 10 products are disapproved and the average approved product generates roughly £342.75 ÷ 100 = £3.43 of spend per day, those 10 products represent ~£34/day of spend that is being redistributed away from them — not lost, but going to your second-choice products rather than your full catalogue. Over a month, that is ~£1,000 of spend on a narrower product pool than intended, which means lower auction diversity and less chance of PMax finding the cheapest conversion path.

A good answer is a screenshot with actual numbers: "180 submitted, 172 approved, 8 disapproved — 3 for image quality, 5 for missing GTIN." An evasive answer is "everything's fine" with no numbers, or redirecting to Google Ads metrics instead of Merchant Center diagnostics. The diagnostics dashboard is a single page with plain numbers. There is no reason not to share it, and reluctance to do so would itself be a data point about how closely the feed is being managed.

**TikTok equivalent:** TikTok Ads has a Catalog (TikTok for Business → Assets → Catalog) that serves the same function — a product feed powering TikTok Shopping Ads. Same principle applies: if a product is not approved in the catalog, it cannot appear in any Shopping ad. Same diagnostic: check the catalog health dashboard for approval rate and disapproval reasons. The feed management layer is the same job on every platform you run Shopping ads on.

**You should now be able to:** ask Daryl for the Merchant Center diagnostics, read the approval rate, spot disapproved products, and understand what each one costs you in missed PMax impressions and potentially cheaper conversions.
