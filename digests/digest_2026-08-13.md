## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO) vs Ad Set Budget Optimization (ABO)

**Callback:** Yesterday you learned about Facebook's three bidding strategies — Highest Volume, Cost Cap, and Bid Cap — which control what happens at each individual auction. Today's question is one level up: who decides how much of your total budget reaches each audience in the first place?

Every Facebook campaign has a budget. That budget can live in one of two places: at the **campaign level** or at the **ad set level**. This distinction — Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO) versus Ad Set Budget Optimization (ABO) — is the single biggest control lever you have over where your money goes. Getting it wrong is the fastest way to burn budget on the wrong audience without realising it.

**Ad Set Budget Optimization (ABO)** means you set a daily budget on each individual ad set. If you have three ad sets — one targeting cold lookalikes, one retargeting website visitors, one hitting your customer list — each gets its own budget. TOF-WEDDING gets £125/day, TEST OCT 2022 gets £90/day, EOI-Multiple gets £80/day. Facebook cannot move money between them. If TOF-WEDDING exhausts its best prospects by 2pm, the remaining budget buys progressively worse people for the rest of the day, while EOI-Multiple might still have cheap high-intent users it cannot reach because its own budget is also capped. The upside of ABO is total control: you know exactly what goes where, and you can cut or scale one audience without touching others. The downside is that you are doing the optimisation, not the algorithm — and on a good day, the algorithm genuinely can find pockets of demand you would miss.

**Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO)** — which Meta now calls "Advantage Campaign Budget" in the interface, though most marketers still say CBO — means you set one daily budget at the campaign level, say £295/day, and Facebook's algorithm decides in real time how to split it across the ad sets underneath. The algorithm watches auction prices and conversion rates second by second and shifts money toward whichever ad set is delivering the cheapest results right now. The theory is sound: the machine sees thousands of signals per auction that you cannot process manually. The reality is that CBO has a well-documented tendency to concentrate spend on the ad set with the **largest audience and cheapest clicks**, which is not always the ad set producing the most revenue. A broad prospecting ad set can eat 80% of the campaign budget because its CPM is low and its audience pool is huge, starving a smaller retargeting audience that converts at five times the rate but has fewer people in it. The algorithm optimises for the cheapest event, not the most profitable event — and those are different things.

**How to tell which one you are using:** Open Ads Manager. Click into the campaign. If you see a toggle labelled "Campaign Budget Optimization" or "Advantage Campaign Budget" switched **on**, and the budget figure sits at the campaign level, you are on CBO. If the toggle is off and each ad set shows its own daily budget, you are on ABO. The toggle is in the campaign edit screen, not hidden in settings. It takes five seconds to check.

**What the failure looks like from the outside:** You tell your marketer to spend £125/day on a campaign, and it spends £186. The total campaign budget might be correct, but the algorithm chose to give your money to a different ad set than you intended. Your daily spend report looks about right in total, but the ad set breakdown reveals a choice nobody approved. This is exactly the diagnostic question hanging over this account right now.

**Worked example from yesterday:**

Yesterday Facebook ran three campaigns: TOF-WEDDING (£186.44), TEST OCT 2022 (£89.53), EOI-Multiple (£77.79) = £353.76 total. Two scenarios:

*If ABO (separate budgets):* TOF-WEDDING spent £186 because someone set its daily budget at or above £186. The fix is mechanical — open the ad set, change the budget field to £125, save. Done in under a minute.

*If CBO (shared budget):* All three sit under one campaign with, say, a £355/day budget. Facebook decided to give TOF-WEDDING £186 and EOI-Multiple £78 from the same pool. The fix is different: you cannot just "tell Facebook" to cap TOF-WEDDING at £125 by default. You need to set an **ad set spend limit** — found in the ad set edit screen under "Budget & Schedule" — which caps how much of the shared pool that ad set can take. Setting a maximum spend of £125 on the TOF-WEDDING ad set within a CBO campaign is the quickest way to enforce a budget decision. Alternatively, you pull TOF-WEDDING out into its own ABO campaign, which is cleaner but resets its learning data.

The cost of not knowing which structure you are using: the 11 Aug digest recommended pulling TOF-WEDDING to ~£125/day. Two days later it has spent £225 then £186. Either the budget was not changed, or it is CBO and the algorithm overrode the intent. Until the structure is confirmed, every budget recommendation in this digest is unenforceable.

On the Google side, Performance Max (PMax) is always CBO-like by design — Google decides how to allocate across asset groups and channels (Shopping, Display, YouTube, Search) within the campaign. You cannot set per-asset-group budgets. The only budget control is the campaign daily budget itself, which is why the recommendation for PMAX CSS DEC is stated as a single campaign-level figure (~£320/day).

**TikTok equivalent:** TikTok Ads Manager has the same CBO toggle (also called "Campaign Budget Optimization"). Its algorithm is less mature than Meta's and even more prone to concentrating spend on the cheapest ad group — worth knowing when a third channel opens up.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "Are TOF-WEDDING, TEST OCT 2022, and EOI-Multiple in the same campaign with Advantage Campaign Budget on, or are they separate campaigns with their own daily budgets? If CBO is on, what are the ad set spend limits?" A good answer is specific: "TOF-WEDDING is its own campaign, ABO, daily budget £200" or "They share a CBO campaign at £350/day with no spend limits set." An evasive answer is "Facebook optimises it automatically" without naming which setting is active — that means he does not know or does not want you to know.

**You should now be able to:** look at any Facebook campaign in Ads Manager and identify whether it uses CBO or ABO, understand why a budget recommendation can fail to land if the campaign structure does not support it, and know that ad set spend limits are the tool for enforcing per-audience caps within a CBO campaign.
