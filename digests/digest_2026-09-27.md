## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**The breakdown effect: why CBO funds the "wrong" ad set.** This builds on CBO vs ABO and on diminishing returns. Facebook's budget system spreads money to wherever the *next* purchase will be cheapest (the marginal cost). It does not look at which ad set has the best *average* cost per purchase (CPP) so far. An ad set with a cheap average has usually already sold to its easiest buyers, so extra money there buys dearer customers. Yesterday CBO moved TOF-MAIN from 30% to 42% of TOF-WEDDING's budget (£48.08 → £76.97), and its CPP rose from £8.01 to £12.83: £28.89 more spend bought the same 6 purchases. Meanwhile test_creatives_2 cost less per purchase (£9.16) on similar money. Looking at the ad-set table and "fixing" CBO by hand, for example moving money to the cheapest-looking ad set, usually makes the blended CPP worse. Judge CBO on the campaign's total CPP over 3+ days, not ad set by ad set on one day. TikTok's campaign budget optimisation behaves the same way. **Ask Daryl this:** "When you raised TOF-WEDDING to £200, did you expect the campaign CPP to rise, and at what campaign-level CPP would you take it back to £150?" A good answer names a number and judges it at campaign level. An evasive one points at a single ad set's CPP. **You should now be able to** explain why a CBO campaign keeps funding an ad set that looks worse on average, and why you shouldn't override it on one day's numbers.
