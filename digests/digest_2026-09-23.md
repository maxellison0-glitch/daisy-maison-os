## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### ABO vs CBO — Who Controls the Budget, You or the Algorithm?

**Callback:** In the Budget Utilisation Rate lesson (18 Sep), you learned to read daily spend against daily budget as a health check. Today we go one level deeper: where that budget actually sits and who decides how it gets split across ad sets.

---

Every Facebook campaign has a structural choice baked in at creation: **ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimisation)** or **CBO (Campaign Budget Optimisation)**. This is not a minor setting — it fundamentally changes who controls the money.

**ABO** means you assign a fixed daily budget to each individual ad set. If you have three ad sets at £50 each, you spend £50/£50/£50 regardless of which ad set is performing. The algorithm optimises *within* each ad set (choosing which people to show ads to, which placements to use), but it **cannot move money between ad sets**. You are the budget allocator.

**CBO** means you assign one budget to the whole campaign — say £150 — and the algorithm decides how to split it across ad sets in real time. If ad set A is converting and ad set B is not, the algorithm can shift £120 to A and £30 to B automatically. The algorithm is the budget allocator.

**How to tell which you're looking at.** In Windsor, the field `campaign_is_adset_budget_sharing_enabled` tells you: `false` = ABO (each ad set has its own locked budget), `true` = CBO (campaign shares one budget across ad sets). Every active Daisy Maison Facebook campaign currently runs **ABO** (all `false`).

**Why this matters — yesterday's live example.**

Take Christmas Static Posts. Two active ad sets, ABO, £30 total campaign budget:

- **test_xmas_creatives:** £10.07 spent → 3 purchases → £3.36 CPP → £86.07 FB revenue
- **test_xmas_creatives2:** £21.37 spent → 0 purchases → infinite CPP → £0 FB revenue

Under ABO, the split was roughly 34/66 — weighted toward the **worse** performer. The ad set budgets locked that allocation in. The algorithm couldn't help.

Under CBO with the same £30 campaign budget, the algorithm would have seen test_xmas_creatives converting and shifted budget dynamically. If £25 had gone to test_xmas_creatives instead of £10, at the same £3.36 CPP, you'd expect roughly 7 purchases instead of 3. Same £30 spent, potentially more than double the conversions.

The arithmetic: £25 ÷ £3.36 = 7.4 expected purchases vs the 3 that actually happened. That's 4 lost purchases, each worth roughly £29 AOV — about £116 in revenue left on the table from one campaign's budget structure alone.

**So why use ABO at all?** Because CBO gives the algorithm full control, and the algorithm optimises for what **you** told it to — which may not be what you actually want. In a creative test (like TOF-WEDDING's three ad sets testing different creative), CBO would instantly pile money into whichever ad set gets the first conversion, starving the others before they've had a fair test. ABO forces equal exposure so you can compare creative performance on a level playing field.

**ABO is for testing. CBO is for scaling proven winners.**

The failure mode of each:
- **ABO kept too long:** you keep feeding budget to losing ad sets because you haven't manually intervened. This is exactly what Christmas showed yesterday — and what TOF-WEDDING is doing by giving test_creatives budget when its CPP is £16.97 vs TOF-MAIN's £12.67.
- **CBO applied too early:** you never discover whether a slow-starting ad set might have converted at scale, because the algorithm killed its budget on day 2 after a rival got an early run of purchases.

**The rule of thumb:** ABO during the test phase (first 7–14 days, or until one ad set clearly wins over 50+ conversion events). Then switch to CBO to let the algorithm scale the winner. The switch is in Campaign Settings → Campaign Budget Optimisation → toggle on. Facebook re-enters a brief learning phase (~3 days) when you switch.

On TikTok Ads (which you'll likely run as a third channel): the equivalent is called **Campaign Budget Optimization** in TikTok Ads Manager — same concept, same toggle, same test-then-scale logic.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "TOF-WEDDING has been running with three ad sets on ABO for weeks. test_creatives_2 has been the dominant acquirer since mid-September, and test_creatives is now at £16.97 CPP. Is there a plan to move TOF-WEDDING to CBO so the algorithm can scale the winner — or are we staying on ABO because new creative is coming?" A good answer names a reason for ABO (active creative rotation planned) or acknowledges the transition is overdue. An evasive answer is "it's fine" without explaining why budget still flows to a £17 CPP ad set when another converts at £12.67.

**You should now be able to** look at any Facebook campaign in Windsor, check `campaign_is_adset_budget_sharing_enabled`, and know instantly whether money moves between ad sets automatically (CBO) or sits locked at the ad set level (ABO) — and judge whether that structure still matches the campaign's maturity.
