## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) — why one order isn't the whole story**

*(Just for you — not for Daryl to see.)*

You check ROAS every day, and rightly so. But ROAS only tells you what a customer is worth on the day they buy. Customer Lifetime Value asks a different question: **what is a customer worth to the business over their entire relationship with you — not just their first order?**

**What CLV is:**

CLV = the total revenue a customer generates across all their purchases, over the full time they remain active. For a seasonal business like Daisy Maison, the useful window is 12 months. The basic formula: **CLV = Average Order Value × how often they buy per year**.

If a customer buys once: CLV = £27.45 (yesterday's AOV). If they come back for Christmas: CLV = £27.45 + £35 = £62.45. Suddenly the economics of acquiring that customer look completely different.

**Why it changes your ROAS thinking:**

Your 3x ROAS floor is the right discipline for daily decisions. But it's calculated on a single purchase. If 12% of customers come back and buy again — which is entirely plausible for a personalised gift brand that people gift for every occasion — then the real return on yesterday's £535.83 of spend includes a share of those future orders.

Yesterday's 50 orders at £27.45 AOV = £1,372 captured immediately. If 6 of those 50 people come back in November for a Christmas personalised gift at £35, that's another £210 in revenue from the same acquisition cost. Your real ROAS on yesterday's spend wasn't 2.56x — it's higher, just deferred.

**Why this matters right now:**

The FD buyers you acquired in May and June — over 1,000 orders in a single peak week — are sitting in your customer database and your Facebook Pixel right now. The question isn't just "did the FD campaign hit 3x?" It's "how many of those buyers come back for Christmas?" That's CLV working in the background.

This is also why Daryl's lookalike audiences work better every season: as your buyer pool grows, the lookalike quality improves, CPM falls, and ROAS on future campaigns rises — without spending more. You're not just buying orders. You're buying a compounding audience.

**Applied to today:**

Yesterday's 50 orders added 50 people to your buyer pool. On a 2.56x day, the instinct is to worry. But if 5–8 of those 50 people spend again before Christmas, yesterday was quietly profitable — just not on the timeline a single-purchase ROAS calculation can see.

The lesson: 3x ROAS is the right daily discipline. But when ROAS dips in a new season's first week, CLV is the reason not to panic and pull all spend. You're not just buying today's orders — you're buying a relationship that has compound value across every future occasion.
