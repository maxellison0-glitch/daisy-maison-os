## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### The spend–ROAS curve: finding your green-zone ceiling

**Callback:** We covered diminishing returns on 28 Jul — each extra pound of ad spend buys a slightly worse customer than the one before. Today's lesson turns that principle into a practical tool: a way to look at your own data and extract a specific number — the daily budget above which you are consistently losing money.

**What a spend–ROAS curve is.** Take every day of the last three or four weeks where you have both total ad spend and Shopify ROAS. Plot them: spend on the horizontal axis, ROAS on the vertical. Each dot is one trading day. Over 20+ days, the dots form a pattern — not random, but a downward-sloping cloud. Low-spend days cluster in the upper-left (high ROAS), high-spend days cluster in the lower-right (low ROAS). Somewhere in the middle of that cloud, the dots cross the 3x ROAS line. That crossing point is your **green-zone ceiling** — the daily budget above which you are red more often than not.

**Why the ceiling exists.** Both Google and Facebook run auctions. When your daily budget is small, the algorithm only needs a handful of high-intent people to spend it: someone who searched "personalised wedding gift" ten minutes ago, or a previous visitor who left without buying. Those people are cheap to reach AND ready to buy — they convert. As you raise the budget, the algorithm goes wider: people who searched "wedding gift ideas" last week, people whose browsing pattern vaguely overlaps with your buyer profile, people in adjacent demographics the algorithm thinks might convert. Each successive cohort is a little less ready, a little more expensive, a little less likely to buy. At some point, the cost of reaching them exceeds what they return in Shopify revenue. That point is the ceiling.

**It moves.** The green-zone ceiling is not a fixed number. It shifts with seasonal demand. During Father's Day week, thousands of people were actively searching for dad gifts — the pool of cheap, high-intent people was huge, so the ceiling moved right and you could spend GBP 750+ and still land green (14 Jun: GBP 1,424 total spend, 3.27x ROAS). In a quiet late-July week, with teacher over and wedding as the only demand pool, the pool of high-intent buyers is smaller, so the ceiling shifts left. A budget that was profitable in June becomes unprofitable in August at the exact same CPC. The demand pool, not the budget, is the variable.

**What it looks like when you miss it.** You don't see one bad day — you see a string of red. Three consecutive sub-3x days at the same spend level that used to produce green is the signature. That's the curve telling you the ceiling moved and your spend hasn't followed. The fix isn't new creative or a different audience — it's a lower budget, held there until demand changes again.

**Worked example — your actual data.** Here are the last 10 days with complete spend and ROAS data, sorted by spend:

| Day | Spend | ROAS | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 20 Mon | GBP 542 | 3.05x | 🟢 |
| Jul 21 Tue | GBP 584 | 3.41x | 🟢 |
| Jul 19 Sun | GBP 631 | 3.00x | 🟢 |
| Jul 27 Mon | GBP 632 | 2.31x | 🔴 |
| Jul 23 Thu | GBP 633 | 2.66x | 🔴 |
| Aug 2 Sun | GBP 666 | 2.71x | 🔴 |
| Jul 25 Sat | GBP 685 | 2.17x | 🔴 |
| Jul 24 Fri | GBP 690 | 2.21x | 🔴 |
| Jul 26 Sun | GBP 735 | 2.46x | 🔴 |
| Jul 28 Tue | GBP 715 | 2.00x | 🔴 |

The line is stark: every green day was at spend **GBP 631 or below**. Every day at GBP 632 or above was red. The green-zone ceiling sits at roughly **GBP 630/day** for the store's current demand pool.

Now the arithmetic. On Jul 21 you spent GBP 584 and got GBP 1,994 total sales (3.41x). On Jul 28 you spent GBP 715 and got GBP 1,430 total sales (2.00x). You spent **GBP 131 more** and got **GBP 564 less** in revenue. The marginal ROAS on that extra GBP 131 was (GBP 1,430 − GBP 1,994) ÷ (GBP 715 − GBP 584) = **negative 4.3x**. Not just unprofitable — every extra pound above the ceiling actively destroyed value.

Yesterday's GBP 666 at 2.71x sits in the red zone but is the best ROAS since the spend came down from GBP 715+. The PMAX cut is dragging total spend back toward the ceiling. To land in the green band, total spend needs to fall another GBP 35–65 to ~GBP 600–630. Cutting PMAX CSS DEC to GBP 300/day (from GBP 348) would bring total spend to roughly GBP 619 — right at the ceiling.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Can you show me PMAX CSS DEC's spend versus return over the last 30 days? I want to see where the break-even sits for that one campaign." A good answer gives you the actual numbers — daily spend, daily Shopify-attributed revenue — and lets you draw the curve yourself. An evasive answer is "the algorithm optimises automatically" or "PMax handles this internally." That's true in a narrow sense but irrelevant: auto-optimisation within a budget doesn't tell you whether the budget itself is too high. You're not questioning the algorithm; you're questioning the input.

**You should now be able to:** sort a fortnight of days by spend, draw a line at 3x ROAS, and say "our green zone ends at roughly GBP X per day" — turning a vague sense of "we're spending too much" into a specific, testable budget target that you can set and verify.
