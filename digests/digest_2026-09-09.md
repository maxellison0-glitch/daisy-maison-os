## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Creative IS Targeting — How Each Ad Image Finds a Different Customer

**Callback:** On 8 Sep you learned the three-gate CPP framework for killing a campaign: Time, Spend, CPP trajectory. That framework assumed you are judging a CAMPAIGN. Today's lesson goes one level deeper — inside the same campaign, why two ad sets with identical audiences produce completely opposite CPP results, and what that tells you about how Facebook's algorithm actually works.

**The concept.** When Facebook shows an ad, it does not broadcast it equally to everyone in the audience definition. It uses the ad IMAGE to predict who will engage. The algorithm processes the visual elements — a room setting, a product on white background, a lifestyle shot, a close-up — and matches those signals to users who historically respond to similar visual content. Two different creatives in the same campaign, targeting the same audience, will be shown to DIFFERENT people because the algorithm routes each image to the slice of the audience most likely to act on it.

This means creative is not just "what the ad looks like." **Creative is a second targeting layer that sits on top of your audience definition.** Your audience says "women 25-55 interested in gifts." Your creative decides WHICH women 25-55 interested in gifts actually see the ad. A lifestyle image of a diffuser in a styled room reaches people who engage with home décor and gifting content. A product photo on white background reaches bargain-hunting comparison shoppers. Same audience. Radically different ACTUAL people reached.

**Why this matters mechanically.** Facebook's ad delivery works through something called estimated action rate (EAR) — the algorithm's prediction of how likely a specific person is to take the action you optimised for (in your case, Purchase). EAR is calculated per impression, per person, per creative. When Facebook has two creatives to choose from, it scores every potential viewer twice — once for each creative — and shows whichever creative has the higher predicted action rate for that particular person. This is why two ad sets can spend similar amounts but deliver to almost non-overlapping groups of people.

The crucial implication: **a cheap CPC does not mean good targeting.** A product photo might generate curiosity clicks from price-comparison shoppers — they see the product, wonder about the price, click, see £14.95, leave. The CPC looks excellent because lots of people click. But the CPP is infinite because none of them buy. An AI lifestyle image might generate fewer clicks at higher CPC — but those clicks come from people who see the room, imagine the gift, and arrive with purchase intent.

**Worked example: TOF-Diffusers, 9 Sep.**

Same campaign. Same audience. Same product (Mr & Mrs Reed Diffuser, £14.95). Same day.

| Ad Set | Spend | Clicks | CPC | Purchases | CPP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| product_images | £27.34 | 79 | £0.35 | 0 | ∞ |
| ai_images | £19.25 | 25 | £0.77 | 3 | £6.42 |

product_images gets **3× more clicks** at **half the CPC**. By every click metric, it wins. But zero purchases. ai_images gets fewer, more expensive clicks — and converts them at a rate that makes it the cheapest acquisition in the entire 14-ad-set portfolio.

The £0.35 clicks from product_images are not "good cheap traffic." They are the wrong people, found by the wrong creative. The £0.77 clicks from ai_images are the right people, found by the right creative.

The arithmetic: product_images burned £27.34 for nothing. If you paused it and redirected that spend to ai_images (total budget £49.85 to ai_images alone), at £6.42 CPP you would project ~8 purchases instead of 3. Diminishing returns would erode that somewhat at scale, but the direction is unambiguous.

**The diagnostic to run on any campaign.** When two ad sets in the same campaign show different CPP:

1. Check whether they target the same audience. If yes → the creative is the variable.
2. Compare CPC to CPP. If one has cheap CPC but bad CPP, it is finding clickers, not buyers.
3. Look at the purchase count, not just the ratio. ai_images has 3 purchases — small sample but combined with yesterday's 3 at £2.02 CPP (day 1), the creative is consistently converting. product_images has had 2 days of meaningful spend and zero purchases total.

**TikTok note:** TikTok's algorithm works similarly — creative determines delivery even more aggressively because TikTok's For You Page prioritises content engagement signals over audience definitions. A creative that gets watched (high view-through rate) will be shown to more people regardless of your targeting. On TikTok, creative IS targeting in an even more literal sense.

**ASK DARYL THIS:** "When you set up TOF-Diffusers with two ad sets, did you vary the creative or the audience between them? And now that product_images has £27 of spend with zero purchases while ai_images has 3 purchases at £6.42, would you pause product_images or give it more time?" A good answer says creative was the variable and pauses the loser immediately — the data is clean enough. An evasive answer talks about "learning phase" without acknowledging that both ad sets had the same time and similar spend, so the difference is the creative, not the data volume.

**You should now be able to:** look at two ad sets inside the same campaign with different CPP results and diagnose whether the gap is caused by the creative or the audience — and make the pause/keep decision on the loser without waiting for more data that will only confirm what you already see.
