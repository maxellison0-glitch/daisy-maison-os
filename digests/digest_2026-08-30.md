## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Auction Insights in Google Ads

**Callback.** On 24 Jun you learned that Impression Share tells you what fraction of eligible auctions your ads actually appear in. But Impression Share treated the competitive landscape as a sealed box — you knew you were missing perhaps 70–80% of auctions, but not who was winning them. Today opens that box.

**What Auction Insights is.** It is a report built into Google Ads that reveals exactly who is competing against you in the same ad auctions — shown by their display domain (the website URL that appears on their Shopping card or text ad) — and how you are performing against each of them. It answers the question Impression Share alone cannot: "Who am I losing to?"

**Where to find it.** In Google Ads, click into a campaign — say, PMAX CSS AUG. In the left navigation, look for "Insights and reports" → "Auction insights." For some interface layouts it appears as a tab alongside "Overview" and "Settings" at campaign level. For PMax campaigns, the report is available at campaign level only. For Standard Shopping campaigns, you can drill into ad group level, which narrows the competitor set to a specific product category and is more useful for diagnosis.

**The five metrics the report shows, and what each one means:**

1. **Impression share** — the percentage of all eligible impressions your ads actually won. You already understand this from 24 Jun. In Auction Insights, it sits next to each competitor's impression share, so you can see the pie sliced up: if you hold 18% and a competitor holds 42%, they capture more than double your share of the same shoppers.

2. **Overlap rate** — how often a specific competitor's ad appeared at the same time as yours. If "competitor-gifts.co.uk" shows 75% overlap, they appear alongside your product card in three out of four of your auctions. High overlap means you are directly competing for the same search queries. Low overlap means the competitor targets a different query set and is not directly in your lane.

3. **Outranking share** — how often your ad ranked higher than a competitor's, or you appeared when they did not. This is the most actionable metric. An outranking share of 35% against a competitor means they beat you 65% of the time when you are both eligible for the same auction. If your outranking share against a key competitor drops week over week, they are gaining ground.

4. **Position above rate** — how often a competitor's ad appeared in a higher position than yours. In Shopping, "higher position" means their product card is closer to the left of the carousel — the first cards a shopper sees. A competitor with a 50% position-above rate is appearing ahead of you in half your shared auctions.

5. **Top of page rate and absolute top of page rate** — how often your ad appeared at the top of search results, and how often it was in the absolute first position. For Shopping, this maps to the first visible row of the product carousel.

**Why it matters operationally.** When CPC rises on a campaign, there are exactly two causes: (a) your budget, bid strategy, or quality changed, or (b) a competitor entered or increased their spend. Change History (taught 25 Aug) diagnoses cause (a). Auction Insights diagnoses cause (b). If a new domain appears with 60%+ overlap and high position-above rate, a competitor has entered your auctions and the auction price has risen accordingly. The correct response is not to raise your budget blindly — that is what Google wants you to do — but to check whether your current impression share still delivers enough volume at your ROAS target, and whether the competitor is actually converting or just overpaying.

**The failure mode from the outside.** Imagine PMAX CPC rises from £0.69 to £0.90 over a fortnight. Without Auction Insights you might assume the algorithm is misbehaving or the learning phase is volatile. With it, you might discover that a domain like "personalised-gifts-uk.co.uk" jumped from 20% to 55% overlap rate — they have started competing for the same personalised-gift shoppers. The auction price rose because two bidders now chase the same impression. Knowing this changes the decision entirely: instead of raising budget into an expensive auction, you check whether your product feed quality (taught 1 Jul) and landing page experience are strong enough to win on relevance and quality score rather than raw bid.

**Worked example.** Yesterday, PMAX CSS AUG spent £224.28 on 325 clicks at £0.69 CPC — the best CPC of the learning phase. If you opened Auction Insights for PMAX CSS AUG filtered to the last 14 days, you would see a table something like:

| Display domain | Impr. share | Overlap rate | Outranking share | Pos. above |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| daisymaisonuk (You) | ~18% | — | — | — |
| competitor-a.co.uk | ~40% | 75% | 35% | 42% |
| competitor-b.com | ~25% | 60% | 48% | 28% |
| competitor-c.co.uk | ~12% | 35% | 58% | 18% |

Your impression share of ~18% means you appear in fewer than 1 in 5 eligible auctions — the budget constraint that £224 daily spend creates. Competitor-a at 40% impression share and 75% overlap is the one taking your share: they appear alongside you in 75% of your auctions and beat you 65% of the time (100% minus 35% outranking share). Competitor-c at 35% overlap and 58% outranking is a weaker player you comfortably outrank. The practical question from here: is competitor-a's dominance stable or growing? Filter the report to the last 7 days versus the prior 7 — if their overlap and impression share are rising week over week, they are scaling spend into your auctions and your CPC will keep rising unless you improve relevance or bid efficiency.

**The PMax limitation.** PMax gives Auction Insights at campaign level only — you cannot break it down by asset group or individual product. Standard Shopping campaigns offer ad-group-level Auction Insights, which narrows the competitor set to a specific product category. This is one reason some advertisers run a parallel Standard Shopping campaign alongside PMax for their hero products: it provides granular competitive intelligence that PMax withholds. For now, the campaign-level view is enough to see who the competitive threat is and whether it is growing.

**TikTok note:** TikTok Ads Manager has no direct equivalent — the auction is opaque. You can see your own impression volume and CPM but not which competitors are bidding against you. This is one of Google's transparency advantages.
