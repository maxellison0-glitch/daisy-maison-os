## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**SPAG: Single Product Ad Groups**

*This builds on the search terms report and negative keywords.* An **ad group** is a box inside a campaign that holds products and shares one bid and one set of negative keywords. A **SPAG** puts **one product in each box**. Why bother? Each product gets its own bid, its own search terms report and its own negatives. So "cheap wooden sign" can be blocked on the Mr & Mrs sign without blocking it anywhere else. PMax gives you none of that: Google pools everything and shows you a total. The cost is that a new campaign starts with no history. Smart Bidding (Google's automatic bidding) has to relearn every product, so expect 1–2 weeks of jumpy numbers. **Failure looks like:** spend concentrates on 2–3 products and the rest get nothing.

**Worked example:** SPAG's first day was £176.21 for 245 clicks = **£0.72 CPC**, the same as PMax AUG's 7-day average (£0.74). It's buying traffic at the same price. Whether that traffic buys is still unknown.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Which products are in SPAG, and are they excluded from ALL OTHER CSS?" A good answer names the products and confirms the exclusions. An evasive one is "Google sorts it."

**TikTok:** catalog ads use *product sets*, which are the nearest equivalent.

**You should now be able to** tell a structure change from a budget change, and know why a rebuild needs two weeks before anyone judges it.
