## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Hook rate: the first number to read on a video ad**

*This builds on "Creative IS targeting" and "Clicks are not visits".* An **impression** is one appearance on someone's screen. A **3-second video play** is when someone watches for at least 3 seconds. **Hook rate** = 3-second plays ÷ impressions: the share of people who stopped scrolling. **Hold rate** = ThruPlays (15s or the full video) ÷ 3-second plays: the share who kept watching. Meta predicts how likely each ad is to get attention and charges less for impressions on ads people stop for. A weak hook has a recognisable failure pattern: CPM rises, the budget goes under-spent, and too few people ever reach the click. Rough rule of thumb: under ~20% is weak, and 30%+ is strong. Fix a weak hook with the first second of the video, not with the audience.

**Worked example:** your Reel got 1,315 impressions for £6.62 → CPM £6.62 ÷ 1.315 = **£5.03**. It got 17 clicks ÷ 1,315 = **1.29% CTR**. Hook rate needs one more column: in Ads Manager, add "3-second video plays" and "ThruPlays". If 300 people stopped, the hook would be 300 ÷ 1,315 = 22.8%.

**Ask Daryl this:** "What hook rate do the TOF video ads get, and below what number do you kill a creative?" Good answer: a number and a cut-off ("under 20% after ~3k impressions"). Evasive answer: "Meta optimises that."

**TikTok:** the same idea is the 2-second and 6-second view rate.

**You should now be able to** tell whether a video ad is failing in the first second or later.
