## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Product groups and custom labels: how a Shopping campaign decides what it's allowed to sell**

*This builds on SPAG (13 Oct) and budget-limited vs bid-limited (Underspend).* A Shopping or PMax campaign doesn't choose products itself. It gets a **product group** (in PMax, a **listing group**): a slice of the Merchant Center feed, cut by category, brand, item ID or **custom label**. Custom labels are five free-text columns in the feed (custom_label_0–4) where you write tags like "bestseller", "high-margin" or "q4". Each product sits in exactly one bidding slice. Anything not carved out falls into the "everything else" catch-all. Move a product's label and it changes campaigns overnight, without anyone touching the campaign. The failure looks like this from outside: budget and bid strategy are unchanged, but impressions halve and spend stalls. The campaign simply has fewer products to enter auctions with.

**Worked example:** SPAG impressions went from 22,176 (13 Oct) to 11,019 (20 Oct), and its spend fell from £224.07 to £69.88 (35% of £200). Over the same days, the "ALL OTHER" catch-all rose from £75.23 to £106.44 (106%), because the products sitting in it changed.

**What to watch for:** one Shopping campaign under-spending while the catch-all over-spends. Claude flags it. TikTok: catalog ads use "product sets", which work the same way.

**You should now be able to** tell a shrunk product list from a bid problem in 30 seconds.
