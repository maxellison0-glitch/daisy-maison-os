## 📚 Lesson of the Day *(confidential: Max only, not Daryl)*

**Dayparting: running ads only on the days and hours that pay**

*This builds on Day-of-week baselines.* **Dayparting** means limiting ads to chosen hours or days. In Google it's set with an **ad schedule**, a list of the times a campaign is allowed to show. The catch is that PMax uses **Smart Bidding** (Google's automatic bidding), which already adjusts bids by day and hour, and it ignores manual "bid −20% on Sundays" settings. So for PMax the only real levers are a hard on/off or a lower budget on that day. Facebook only allows schedules on ad sets with a *lifetime* budget. TikTok has dayparting built into ad group settings. **You need dayparting when** the same day comes back below break-even week after week while it takes full spend.

**Worked example:** on Sunday 4 Oct, £652.29 of spend returned 1.90x. On Sunday 11 Oct, £519.74 returned 1.91x. Sunday's extra £143.49 over Saturday returned 0.80x. Before that, Sundays 27 Sep and 20 Sep were 2.41x and 2.74x. **Two weeks is a hint, not proof.**

**Ask Daryl this:** "Sundays have run around 1.9x for two weeks. Is there a schedule or Sunday budget rule, and would you trial a lower Sunday budget for 3 weeks?" A good answer points to data, for example that Sunday browsers buy on Monday. An evasive one is "PMax optimises itself."

**You should now be able to** tell a day-of-week pattern that needs a schedule from one that's just noise.
