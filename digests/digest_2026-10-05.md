## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**PMax Channel Performance: where Performance Max actually spends your money.**

*Builds on: Google PMax (7 Jun) and CPC vs CPM (17 Jul).* That lesson called PMax one campaign. This one opens it up to show where the money goes.

**What it is.** Performance Max (PMax) is one Google campaign that can show ads in six places, which Google calls *channels*:
- **Shopping**: the product cards with price and image on the search results page.
- **Search**: text ads above the results.
- **Display**: banner ads on other websites and apps.
- **YouTube**: video ads.
- **Gmail** and **Discover**: ads in the inbox and in the Google app feed.

You don't choose the split. Google's bidding algorithm moves money between channels every auction to hit its goal: as much conversion value as possible from the budget.

**Why it misbehaves.** Each channel has a different *intent*, meaning how close the viewer is to buying. A Shopping click on "personalised mr and mrs sign" comes from someone already shopping. A Display click comes from someone reading an article who tapped a banner. Display and YouTube impressions are much cheaper, so when PMax is given more budget, or loses easy Shopping auctions, it can fill the gap with cheaper inventory.

Seen from outside, the signature is:
1. Spend up.
2. Impressions up more than spend.
3. CPM and CPC *down*.
4. Clicks and sessions up.
5. Conversion rate down.

Every efficiency number looks better, and the business result looks worse.

**Worked example (4 Oct):** PMAX AUG spent **£401.85** against a 7-day average of £304.80 (+32%). Impressions were 54,597 against 38,633 (+41%). CPM fell from £7.89 to £7.36, and CPC fell from £0.77 to £0.73. Google Search sessions rose by 119 to 484, but store CVR fell from 4.41% to 3.91%. The extra £97 bought about 158 extra clicks (554 vs 396) at roughly £0.61 each. Orders were up only 3.7 on average. That matches all five steps of the signature. **It's a hypothesis until you check the channel split.** Windsor's campaign-level pull doesn't break PMax out by channel.

**Where to look.** In Google Ads, open the PMax campaign → **Insights & reports → Channel performance**. It shows spend, conversions and conversion value per channel. Compare the last 5 days against 24–29 Sep. If Display/YouTube's share of spend jumped after 30 Sep, the 30 Sep change pushed PMax into low-intent inventory. **TikTok note:** TikTok's equivalent is *placements* (TikTok vs Pangle, its app network). Pangle is the Display trap, so switch it off for gift ecommerce.

**Ask Daryl this:** "In PMAX AUG's Channel performance report, what share of spend went to Shopping vs Display/YouTube for 30 Sep–4 Oct compared with the week before, and what changed on 30 Sep?" A **good answer** gives percentages and names the change (budget, ROAS target, new asset group). An **evasive answer** is "PMax optimises across channels, it's fine."

**You should now be able to** tell from spend, impressions and CPM whether PMax is buying cheaper, lower-intent reach, and find the one report in Google Ads that confirms it.
