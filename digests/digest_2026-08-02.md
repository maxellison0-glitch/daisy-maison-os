## 📚 Lesson of the Day

### Branded vs Non-Branded Search — Why Some of Your Google Traffic Is Already Yours

**CALLBACK:** When we covered demand capture vs demand creation (20 Jul), the lesson was that Google Shopping captures existing search intent while Facebook creates discovery demand. Today peels one layer deeper inside that Google "capture" bucket — because not all captured demand is equal.

Every search query that triggers your Shopping ad falls into one of two buckets.

**Branded search** means someone typed your store name — "Daisy Maison", "daisymaisonuk", "daisy maison wedding sign" — into Google. They already know you exist. They were probably going to buy from you whether your ad appeared or not. Google shows them your Shopping card, they click, they buy, and PMAX takes credit for the conversion.

**Non-branded search** means someone typed a generic product term — "personalised wedding gift", "mr and mrs sign", "pebble picture gift" — without naming your store. These people might never have found you without the ad. When they click and convert, that conversion is genuinely incremental — your ad caused it to happen.

**Why this matters for your store right now:** Performance Max blends both types into a single ROAS number. It does not separate them by default. So when you see PMAX CSS DEC spending £328.67 yesterday and contributing to a 2.25x blended ROAS, some of that return came from branded clicks that would have happened for free as organic search results, and some came from non-branded clicks that the ad genuinely won. The blended number overstates the value of the ad spend because it takes credit for revenue you would have earned anyway.

**The mechanism — why the algorithm does this:** Google's Smart Bidding inside PMAX is optimised to hit its performance target. It knows branded queries convert at much higher rates — often 8–15% compared to 1–4% for generic terms. So the algorithm deliberately bids on your brand name because it is easy money for its own scorecard. Every branded conversion it captures inflates the campaign's reported ROAS, making PMAX look more efficient than it truly is at finding new customers.

This is not a scam or a bug. It is rational behaviour from an algorithm whose job is to maximise conversions within a budget. But it means the headline ROAS from PMAX is always rosier than the prospecting ROAS — the part that actually grows your customer base.

**How to see it in the data:** In Google Ads, go to Campaigns → select PMAX CSS DEC → Insights → Search Categories (or Search Terms, depending on account access). Google now shows the top search categories and, in many accounts, the actual search terms that triggered your ads. You are looking for:

- How many impressions and clicks land on terms containing "Daisy Maison", "daisymaisonuk", or exact product names people would only search if they already knew the store
- What percentage of conversions those branded terms account for
- The conversion rate on branded terms (expect 8–15%) versus non-branded (expect 1–4%)

If branded terms account for, say, 25% of clicks but 50% of conversions, that tells you half of PMAX's reported conversions are people who already knew you. The true prospecting ROAS on the remaining non-branded portion is roughly half the blended figure.

**Worked example with yesterday's numbers:**

PMAX CSS DEC spent **£328.67** for **496 clicks**. Suppose 25% of those clicks — about 124 — were branded. If branded clicks convert at 12% and non-branded at 2.5%:

- Branded: 124 clicks × 12% = ~15 orders
- Non-branded: 372 clicks × 2.5% = ~9 orders
- Total PMAX-attributed orders: ~24

PMAX reports all 24 as its own. But those 15 branded conversions would likely have happened organically — or through a tiny branded search campaign costing pennies. The true incremental conversions from PMAX prospecting are closer to 9. The blended PMAX ROAS looks like it is carrying its weight; the prospecting-only ROAS, once you strip out branded, is materially lower.

This does not mean turn PMAX off — it genuinely does find new customers. But it is a strong reason to be cautious about reading PMAX's reported ROAS as the whole truth. And it is the single most important diagnostic for whether the CSS partner behind the [RDD] campaigns is worth whatever fee Daryl's layer charges, because the CSS advantage (the ~20% click uplift from the CSS badge, taught 4 Jun) only matters on non-branded queries where you are competing for attention. On branded queries, you would win anyway.

I want to see the branded versus non-branded split." A good answer gives you actual numbers — ideally a screenshot or export of the Search Terms/Categories tab.
