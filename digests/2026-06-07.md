## 📚 Lesson of the Day — Google Performance Max (PMax)

**What it is**

Performance Max — PMax — is Google's fully automated campaign type. You already know regular Search campaigns (where you pick keywords, write ads, and Google shows them when someone searches those terms). PMax is different: you hand Google a bundle of assets — headlines, descriptions, images, logos, videos, a URL — and then Google decides where, when, and to whom to show those assets across *all* of its properties at once: Google Search, Google Shopping, YouTube, Gmail, the Display Network (banners on websites), and Google Maps. One campaign, every surface, Google's AI driving all of it.

**The "asset group" concept**

Inside a PMax campaign, you organise your creative inputs into what Google calls *asset groups*. Think of an asset group as a themed bundle: assets for your Large Street Sign, maybe a separate one for Football Stadium Signs, another for Father's Day gifts in general. Google mixes and matches the headlines and images within each group to find the combinations that perform best for the audience it's targeting. It's constantly running micro-experiments on your behalf — and it learns fast.

**Why it's typically stable**

Unlike Facebook campaigns, which reset their learning whenever you change budgets, audiences, or creative, a mature PMax campaign has built up a large bank of historical signals — who clicked, who bought, at what time of day, from which device, with which search query. Google uses all of that to allocate budget in real time. Once those signals are strong, the campaign becomes relatively stable and self-managing. The AI is doing most of the work.

**Now look at your account**

Your biggest Google campaign right now is `[RDD] TOP PRODUCTS - PMAX CSS - DEC`. Yesterday it spent **£420.19** and delivered **579 clicks at £0.69 each**. That's your PMax. It's doing the heavy lifting on Google — outspending the second campaign (£71.46) by nearly 6x.

You'll notice the name has two things tacked on: **CSS** and **DEC**. You learned on 4 June what CSS means — the Comparison Shopping Service overlay that gives your Shopping ads a ~20% discount on click costs. So this campaign is a PMax *with* a CSS layer on top, meaning Google is running it through the CSS partner's account to get cheaper Shopping clicks. The "DEC" is likely when it was originally set up (December), not something operational you need to worry about.

The "[RDD]" prefix in the name is also significant — that's Daryl's naming convention for campaigns managed through his agency's infrastructure (or a partner's). This is the CSS partner dependency that's still unresolved: who is the CSS partner, what's the fee arrangement, and could you maintain access to this if Daryl were no longer managing the account? That question doesn't need answering today, but it's the one piece you'll need clarity on before going fully in-house.

**The practical upshot**

When you look at Google's performance and something looks odd — spend dipping, clicks dropping — the most likely culprit is the PMax campaign, because it's driving 85%+ of Google's volume. If PMax is steady, Google is steady. And unlike Facebook (where you can watch campaigns visibly "resetting" after changes), PMax tends to absorb disruption more quietly. It won't panic at a one-day anomaly. That's both its strength and its occasional blind spot — it can mask a slow drift in performance if you're not watching the trend over a few weeks.
