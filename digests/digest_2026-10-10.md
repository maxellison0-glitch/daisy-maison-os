## 📚 Lesson of the Day

**Primary vs secondary conversion actions: what the algorithm is actually chasing**

*Builds on Smart Bidding and Standard Events/the Pixel.* A **conversion action** is any event you tell Google or Meta to count, such as a purchase, add-to-cart or checkout start. In Google Ads each one is set as **Primary**, which Smart Bidding optimises toward and which shows in the "Conversions" column, or **Secondary**, which is recorded for reporting only. Bidding learns from Primary actions alone. So if add-to-cart is set as Primary, Google treats a cart-add like a sale. It then buys cheap browsers who add to cart and leave, and its reported ROAS goes up while Shopify's goes down. **From the outside, the failure looks like this:** platform conversions far above Shopify orders, and platform ROAS rising while store ROAS falls.

**Worked example:** yesterday had 54 cart sessions and 24 completed checkouts. If cart-add were Primary, Google would see 78 "conversions", 3.25× the real 24, and would bid as if every click were worth about 3× more.

**TikTok:** it uses the same idea, called the "optimization event" on the ad group.

**Ask Daryl this:** "Which conversion actions are set to Primary in the Daisy Google account, and is Purchase the only one?" A good answer is "Purchase only, from the Shopify/Google channel tag." An evasive one is "the standard ones."

**You should now be able to** check whether Google is optimising for sales or for something that only looks like sales.
