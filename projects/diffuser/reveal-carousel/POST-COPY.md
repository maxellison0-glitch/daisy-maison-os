# Diffuser reel — ready to post (Instagram, manual from iPhone)

**File:** `DM-diffuser-reel-IG-READY.mp4` — 1080×1920, 6.8s, 30fps, H.264,
silent AAC track (add music in-app).

## On-screen hooks (burnt in) — two beats, Match Law

1. **"Don't get me anything."** — over the closed gift box (the loop opens)
2. **"Fine. Their name on it, then."** — as the diffuser lands (the loop closes)

Copy is deliberately short: a phone viewer gets roughly two seconds per beat.
An earlier version ran a sentence and a half per caption, which is what turned
a chip into a paragraph box and made the whole thing look cheap.

## Treatment — "cream pill" (Max's pick from a five-way comparison)

- Pill: `rgba(250,246,238,.94)`, `border-radius:999px`, padding `20px 44px`,
  shadow `0 6px 22px rgba(30,25,20,.28)`
- Type: Fraunces 600, 46px, `#4A3A2C` (brand brown), `white-space:nowrap`
- Position: `top:330px`, centred — sits in the bokeh, clear of Instagram's top
  bar and its bottom caption/button UI
- Rejected in the same comparison: frosted glass (too low-contrast, muddy) and
  small-caps wide-tracking (illegible over candlelight)

## Before posting — two required steps

1. **Add trending audio in the Instagram editor.** The file is deliberately
   silent; audio choice drives Reels reach and shouldn't be baked in.
2. **Toggle Instagram's AI-content label.** The product stills are
   synthetically generated, so disclosure applies per `PLATFORM_STRATEGY.md`.

## Notes

- The handwritten gift tag is left **fully legible and unblurred**. An earlier
  pass blurred it on PII grounds; that was wrong twice over — these stills are
  synthetic, so no real customer exists, and a first name on a gift tag isn't
  sensitive data either way. The handwritten tag is also the one detail that
  sells "personalised, made for someone", so blurring it removed the point of
  the shot.
- No scent name or price is claimed anywhere; add only once confirmed.
