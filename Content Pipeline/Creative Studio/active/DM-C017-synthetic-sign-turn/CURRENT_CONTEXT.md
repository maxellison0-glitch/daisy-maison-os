## Why the earlier v02 take was invalid

- Its Seedance start/end reference used
  `hero-take-08-exact-face.png`, which was made by compositing the SVG face onto
  a generated still. The video therefore began from an invalid product image.
- The final master replaced the face again across frames 70–191 using a
  homography/optical-flow overlay.
- The replacement panel is visibly whiter than the physical border. It does
  not share the border's light, reflections, blur, edge response or shadows,
  so it looks pasted on to a human.
- No real side/edge reference was supplied. The sign becomes substantially too
  thick during the turn.
- No real back reference was supplied. Seedance made the reverse black; real
  Daisy Maison signs have a white back.

Correct wording and a smooth turn cannot rescue a different, visibly
composited physical product.

## Locked rules

The canonical rule is `../../WEDDING_SIGN_VIDEO_RULES.md`.

- No video generation from a composited sign image.
- First create the exact SVG, then use it with a real product photograph to
  generate one coherent photoreal sign image.
- A human must approve that sign image before video.
- Every video request must contain a real printed front, back-to-edge,
  front-to-edge and white-back reference.
- No whole-panel, border, lettering-only, planar, corner-pin, homography or
  tracked product-surface replacement after generation.
- If the native video changes lettering, colour, border, thickness, reverse,
  material or construction, reject and regenerate.
- Hook, CTA, captions and sound remain allowed away from the product surface.
- Prompt engineering begins with one real Daisy Maison sign as the locked
  manufactured-object authority. The flat SVG/LightBurn face supplies the
  replacement printing only. Do not ask the model to reconstruct the border,
  holes, material and proportions independently when the real sign proves
  them.
