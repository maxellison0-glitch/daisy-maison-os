# DM-C020 — The Daisy Maison office, remodelled

**What this is.** A written map of the real unit, and the exact brief for the
version we generate. Max, 28 Jul 2026: *"Everything needs to be detailed and
listed. Otherwise, it'll just create a weird generation."* That is the whole
reason this file exists. A generator asked for "a nicer workshop" invents a
different building. A generator handed an inventory keeps ours.

**What it is for.** Once the plates are approved they become the locked
backgrounds for Freya and Alan — the room that makes them read as two people who
work at the same company rather than two unrelated generations. After that, any
street sign can be shot in it, and if a wording wants a different setting the
background can be swapped while the product and the people stay put.

**Source photographs:** `source/real-office-photos/`, taken by Max 28 Jul 2026.

---

## The building, as it actually is

A small UK light-industrial end-of-terrace trade unit. Not a studio, not a
"maker space" — a working unit with a van outside. Two floors: the ground floor
is production and packing, a staircase on the right-hand side runs up to a
mezzanine office.

| Element | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Ceiling** | Exposed galvanised steel profile deck, deep trapezoidal ribs, running front-to-back. Unpainted, visibly industrial. Part-height white soffit under the mezzanine. |
| **Walls** | Painted white blockwork. Mortar courses clearly visible — this is the room's strongest texture cue and must survive. |
| **Floor** | Pale sealed concrete, near-white, matte. Currently marked, scuffed and dusty. |
| **Lighting** | Suspended LED/fluorescent battens on the steel, plus daylight from the open shutter and two small windows. Flat, even, cool-neutral. No modelling, no spill pools. |
| **Front** | Electric roller shutter with visible motor, chain and torsion spring gear. Beside it a glazed personnel door. Vehicles (a blue van, a dark pickup) on block paving outside. |
| **Stairs** | Straight flight, right-hand wall, galvanised tube handrail on stanchions. Currently **dark blue-grey, heavily chipped**, plaster splashes on the treads. |
| **Benches** | Timber-framed worktables with **grey carpet/felt tops**. Chest height. That felt top is a real detail and it is why signs sit flat without scratching. |
| **Shelves** | Plain timber boards on white steel brackets, wall-mounted at head height. |
| **Sign display** | A row of finished customer street signs mounted high on the front beam, and more on the machine-room wall. This is the single most brand-specific feature in the building. |

## The remodel brief

Same building. Same layout. Same machines. Everything else brought up to a
standard the business is about to build anyway.

### 1. Declutter — this does most of the work

Remove entirely: flat-pack cardboard stacks and off-cuts, the blue and yellow
bulk laundry bag, the plastic wheelie bins and swing bin, loose brown packing
paper on the floor, the FRAGILE-taped box towers, the leaning glass/aluminium
panels, the cordless blower and tool batteries, the coconut water carton and
drinks bottles, the kitchen-roll tower, the old white printer under the bench,
and every loose off-cut on the floor.

Keep, because a working room with nothing in it looks fake: **one** tidy stack
of flat sign blanks squared off on a bench, a small run of kraft mailers, and a
single stack of finished signs face-down. Aim for *"tidied for a photograph"*,
not *showroom*.

### 2. Surfaces

- **Floor:** the same pale sealed concrete, resurfaced — even, matte, clean, no
  stains or scuffs. Not polished, not epoxy-glossy. It must not become a
  showroom floor.
- **Walls:** same white blockwork, freshly painted. **Keep the mortar courses
  visible.** A flat wall loses the building.
- **Ceiling:** unchanged. The galvanised profile deck stays exactly as it is —
  it is the most recognisable thing in every frame.
- **Benches:** same timber frames and grey felt tops, cleaned up, edges tidy.

### 3. The stairs — Daisy Maison sage

Max, 28 Jul: *"the stairs were painting daisy Maison sage!"*

- Risers and stringer: **sage `#9AA192`** — the real production colourway, read
  from `projects/daisy-street-sign/production/product-rules.json`, not invented.
- Treads: a slightly deeper tone of the same sage for wear, or bare sealed
  timber. No chips, no plaster splash.
- Handrail: the same galvanised tube, cleaned.
- Understairs: emptied and closed off flush.

### 4. A touch of Claude

A *touch*. Accent, never a scheme — the room is a Daisy Maison room.

| Use | Colour |
|---|---|
| Claude clay/terracotta accent | `#D97757` |
| Claude paper (soft warm off-white) | `#F0EEE6` |
| Claude ink (near-black) | `#191919` |

Where it is allowed to appear, and nowhere else:
- One **clay-terracotta powder-coated stool or step** near the packing bench
- Kraft-and-clay **storage boxes** on the timber shelves, squared off
- A single narrow **clay stripe** on the mezzanine soffit edge, above the stairs
- **Warm off-white** replacing the cold white on the mezzanine soffit only

Not allowed: coloured walls, coloured floor, coloured machines, branded signage
on the walls, a logo painted on anything.

### 5. Planting and warmth

The real room already has two small pothos/monstera plants — keep them, in plain
white pots. Add at most one more. Nothing tropical, nothing large.

### 6. Light

Keep it as it is: flat, even, cool-neutral daylight from the open shutter plus
the battens. **Do not add golden hour, do not add rim light, do not add haze.**
The house standard is a phone snapshot of a real room. Cinematic lighting is the
single fastest way to make this read as an advert instead of a workplace.

## Hard rules for every generation

1. **No readable text anywhere.** Books, labels, notices and posters come back
   as gibberish lettering that survives a thumbnail and dies in a screenshot
   (`prompts/CHARACTER-LOCK-SYSTEM.txt`). The finished street signs on display
   stay as **shapes and silhouettes** — mounted edge-on, face-away, or too far
   to read. When a sign must be readable it gets composited later from a real
   plate, never generated.
2. **No people.** These are empty-room plates. Freya and Alan get added
   afterwards, against their own locks. A person generated into the plate is a
   third character nobody approved.
3. **No mounting holes** on any sign visible in frame — Max, 28 Jul: *"We're not
   doing holes."* Note the real photographs **do** show holes, so they arrive by
   inheritance unless the prompt excludes them.
4. **Same camera position as the source photograph.** This is a remodel, not a
   redesign. If the viewpoint moves, the plates stop being a map.
5. **The machines are copied, not reinterpreted.** If a machine comes back a
   different colour, a different shape, or in a different place, the take is
   rejected regardless of how good the rest looks.
6. **No customer PII.** One source photo had a person at the top of the stairs;
   they are masked in the copy held here, and the unmasked frame is not used. A
   real surname *as sign wording* is the product and is fine; a face is not.
