HOUSE STANDARD — THE PHONE-SNAPSHOT PASS
Applies to EVERY image with a person in it, from 25 Jul 2026, until Max says otherwise.

Max's instruction: "I think you should do that for probably everything until I say
otherwise. iPhone or whatever you use."

WHY THIS EXISTS
---------------
Max passed a polished studio-quality image of Freya holding a sign and then said:
"it looks like AI still because it looks too good, so we have to almost tone down."

He was right, and the cause is specific. A rendered image is missing the things
that GO WRONG in a real photograph. Flawlessness is the tell. Both faults he has
caught are absences, not errors:

  - absent ink contrast   -> vector black stays #000, real ink never does
  - absent camera defect  -> no vignette, no noise, no missed focus, no blemish

So the fix is always to ADD FLAWS, never to add quality.

Tested side by side on the same scene, same sign, same person, only the camera
changed. Max's verdict: the phone snapshot won. "Number three just has a slightly
more real effect, which is interesting because you put 'phone snapshot'."

THE SINGLE MOST EFFECTIVE DETAIL
--------------------------------
Put the focus on the SIGN, not the face. A real person photographing a sign
focuses on the sign; an advertising photographer focuses on the model. That one
choice shifts believability more than the grain and the vignette combined.

THE BLOCK — paste into any prompt with a person in it
----------------------------------------------------

Photograph this as an ordinary snapshot taken on a modern mobile phone by a family
member, NOT as a commercial photograph.

- Unretouched skin. Visible pores, some unevenness, faint shine on the forehead
  and nose, ordinary under-eye tone, small blemishes or irregular freckling. Do
  NOT smooth, slim or beautify the face.
- Hair slightly imperfect: a few loose strands out of place, one strand across the
  shoulder, not styled.
- Ordinary mixed indoor light, slightly cooler and flatter than studio light. Mild
  colour cast from the window. Nothing dramatic or sculpted. No rim light.
- Real phone-lens character: gentle corner vignette, very slight chromatic
  fringing on high-contrast edges, mild barrel distortion, corners marginally
  softer than the centre.
- Visible luminance noise in the shadows and darker parts of the room, as a phone
  sensor produces indoors.
- A trace of hand-held motion blur. FOCUS SHARPEST ON THE PRODUCT, marginally
  soft on the face.
- Framing casually imperfect: horizon a fraction off level, subject not exactly
  centred, uneven headroom.
- Expression relaxed and ordinary rather than posed. Small natural half-smile,
  eyes not perfectly symmetrical.

AVOID: beauty retouching, skin smoothing, studio lighting, rim light, catalogue
polish, HDR look, oversaturation, perfect symmetry.

WHAT THIS BLOCK MUST NEVER TOUCH
--------------------------------
The product. Wording, colourway, border geometry, silhouette, size and position in
frame all stay exactly as locked. This pass changes the CAMERA, not the sign. Run
it as an edit on an already-approved image ("MAKE EXACTLY ONE CHANGE — THE
CAMERA") rather than folding it into the original generation, so the product
cannot drift while the polish comes off.

And still no mounting holes: SIGN_HOLES=0.

LINE 2 IS ALWAYS REGULAR WEIGHT. Never bold.
Tested and rejected 25 Jul 2026 — "the bold makes the text way too big. I would
definitely take it back to the non-bold for everything on line two." It also matches
the real product: both live landing-page heroes show line 2 in regular weight.

SIGN_STRETCH is a judgement call, not a default. Use it where a short wording leaves
an obviously empty plate (MUM'S TAXI sat at 71% of the plate width), but the live
listing photos show line 1 at its natural width, so do not apply it blindly.
