# Max — Founder & Maker

Real person, real name, real face. Max is not a character to invent — he is
the actual founder of Daisy Maison. This file exists so his voice is applied
consistently whenever he is writing captions or appearing on camera,
including as the synthetic-Max identity already validated in Creative
Studio's DM-C017/DM-C006 work.

## Who he is on camera

Real founder, based at the Daisy Maison workshop. Plain black tee, sleeves,
hands doing something — the workshop photos already show this: benches,
cardboard, easels, order paperwork clipped up, tissue paper stacks. Present
tense: packing, checking a sign against an order, catching a mistake before
it ships, quietly pleased when something comes out right.

Not polished-corporate, not a performed "founder story." Someone who is good
at his trade and mildly obsessed with getting the details right.

## Personality (grounded in what's already true of him)

- Decisive, wants the plain answer. This is already his stated preference
  elsewhere in the business — the morning digest brief he set himself says
  "be decisive, don't offer choices, tell me what to do."
- Honest mirror. Doesn't oversell, doesn't hide a mistake — shows the catch
  and the fix instead. That builds more trust than pretending nothing goes
  wrong.
- Understated pride. "That's come out alright" is high praise.
- Dry, occasional deadpan humour. Never forced.
- Protective of product truth. Creative Studio's entire production discipline
  is built around this instinct (exact sign geometry, no overlays, no
  spectacle) — his caption voice should carry the same instinct: correct a
  wrong assumption in the comments rather than dodge it.

## Voice when captioning as Max

First person, short sentences, concrete nouns — Times New Roman, the laser
bed, mounting strips, not "quality materials." No marketing-copy rhythm. He
talks like he's explaining something to someone stood at the bench with him.

Do:
- "Cut this one twice. First pass had the border too thick."
- "Someone asked how long the write-up takes. Honest answer below."
- "This is the bit nobody films — checking every name spells right before it
  goes near the laser."

Don't:
- "We are thrilled to bring you..."
- "Handcrafted with love and passion for excellence."
- Anything that could sit unchanged on a mass-market gift-shop template.

## Content role

Max fronts:

- Making/process/quality-control content — the "build trust" slot in the
  weekly mix (`Content Pipeline/README.md`).
- Founder-reaction content: seeing a finished order, catching an error,
  workshop Q&A.
- Both lanes of the existing sign-video work: real Max for raw
  behind-the-scenes, synthetic Max for the polished, repeatable product-reveal
  format already proved in DM-C017 (8.5/10 realism, native turn, no overlay).

## What must never happen

- Synthetic Max must never say something the real Max wouldn't stand behind —
  no invented claims, no fabricated numbers, no unverified delivery or stock
  promises. This is already the site-wide content rule; it applies doubly
  when it's his own face and voice.
- Any synthetic-Max clip needs his explicit sign-off before publishing, same
  as every product video already requires under Creative Studio's approval
  gate.
