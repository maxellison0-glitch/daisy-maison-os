# DM-C006 — Codex + Claude Opus 4.8 joint review

Date: 21 July 2026
Decision status: research conclusion; nothing published

## Executive decision

DM-C006 v3 is the locked clean visual baseline. Its simplicity is a strength.
Do not add reaction footage, craft shots, testimonials, price claims or more
animation to this 11-second picture.

The one next test is a sound-design pass against the locked frames. Human
sentiment and physical craft become separate recurring content pillars.

## What this advert already does well

- Daisy Maison and the product appear immediately.
- The viewer understands personalisation without sound.
- The Windsor-to-Potter interaction creates a clear before/after story.
- The hard cut connects the order action to the wedding outcome.
- The cream field, restrained copy and limited motion feel premium.
- The 11-second 9:16 construction can function as organic content and a paid
  conversion asset.

## The only finishing gap in this asset

Sound. The visual timing should remain frozen while the following are built:

- a restrained selection tick;
- delete/backspace texture;
- six frame-synchronised name-entry taps;
- one confident transition cue on the hard cut;
- natural wedding air entering on that same frame;
- one repeatable three- or four-note Daisy sonic phrase resolving on the end
  card.

The story must still work muted. Sound should add tactility, reality and memory,
not explain the advert or make it louder.

## What not to force into the baseline

- a giver or recipient reaction;
- real hand/macro/craft footage;
- voiceover or a customer review;
- product claims, urgency, price or delivery language;
- more decorative visual branding;
- deliberately rough creator styling.

These may all have value elsewhere. In this cut they would compete with the
personalisation mechanic and remove the restraint Max responded to.

## The Daisy Sentiment Engine

Every future sentiment piece uses the same reproducible structure:

1. **Emotional premise** — one specific person, relationship and occasion.
2. **Proof object** — the exact real product or verified deterministic artwork.
3. **Human beat** — one small credible action or response.
4. **Safe CTA** — a simple product-relevant invitation with no invented claim.

| Product family | Emotional premise | Proof object | Human beat | Safe CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Street signs | Their wedding or new home receives a name | Exact sign → real sign in context | A hand settles or straightens it | Add their names |
| Pebble Hearts | A person, bond or memory carried close | Real Pebble Heart in a real palm | Fingers close around it; a held breath | Make it theirs |
| Pebble pictures | A shared memory becomes tangible | Real framed picture | A hand tilts it into the light | Their story, in a frame |
| Gift boxes | The giving moment begins before the contents | Real box and contents | Hands lift the lid | Send the whole box |

The first separate sentiment pilot should eventually be the Pebble Heart in a
real hand: one object, one human action and minimal capture complexity.

## Capability map

| Creative requirement | Correct Daisy capability |
|---|---|
| Exact changing names and artwork | Deterministic renderer |
| Wedding atmosphere and verified-source reveal | Higgsfield, subject to Max's realism gate |
| Close product texture, weight, finish, hands or packaging | Small deliberate real phone capture |
| Foley, music, sonic signature and neutral branded narration | ElevenLabs/audio tools plus deterministic edit timing |
| Customer words, voice note, review or reaction | Genuine customer source only |

Higgsfield is not to be used for macro craft proof. It may create atmosphere or
animate a verified source, but it must not invent the product's lettering,
texture, weight or a customer response.

## Ethical automation boundary

The governing test is simple:

> If a generated element asserts a fact about a real person's experience, it
> is not allowed.

Permitted:

- art-directed hands placing or holding a product;
- generated atmosphere that does not pose as evidence;
- movement applied to a verified product source;
- branded foley, music or neutral narration.

Not permitted:

- AI tears or a synthetic crying customer;
- fabricated voice notes, reviews, quotes or gratitude;
- an ElevenLabs voice presented as a real recipient;
- generated close-ups presented as proof of product finish or scale.

Sentiment should be engineered through specificity and setup. Emotion presented
as customer evidence must be real.

## Organic-to-paid operating model

1. Publish clean 9:16 organic masters and purposeful hook variants.
2. Judge winners by three-second hold, completion, saves, shares and comments
   naming a real intended recipient or occasion. Views alone are insufficient.
3. Promote the proven organic post using the platform's native paid format.
4. Derive paid modules from the same approved source:
   - 6-second reveal/product bumper;
   - 11-second clean conversion story;
   - 20–30 second customer story only when genuine customer material exists.
5. Preserve a clean picture master and separate music, foley and voice stems so
   every cutdown can be rebuilt without regenerating the product.

## Reference lessons retained

- Not On The High Street: specific real recipient language and camera-roll
  evidence earn sentiment before the product reveal.
- Biscuiteers: real hands and real craft proof earn permission for playful
  animation; packaging completes the gifting story.
- Papier/TikTok: native in-house and creator content can be tested organically
  and promoted as Spark Ads when it proves itself.
- Luxe Bureau/Ralph Lauren: premium value comes from controlled pacing,
  material close-ups, light, ritual and disciplined music—not more copy.
- Goldsmiths: one source idea should yield longer stories, shorter stories and
  six-second product bumpers rather than one universal edit.

## Resolved disagreement

Codex initially described human meaning, physical craft, sonic memory, brand
assets and CTA specificity as parallel gaps. Opus correctly separated asset
completion from new pillar development:

- sound is the only blocker for this baseline;
- human meaning and craft are separate pillars;
- `ADD THEIR NAMES` is not broken and may be tested later, not replaced now;
- Daisy's calm visual grammar should become more ownable through a sonic
  signature and consistent reveal structure, not added visual furniture.

Codex accepts that distinction.

## Next action when Max chooses to continue

Design and render one sound pass against the locked v3 picture. Change no
visual frames and test no second variable in the same version.
