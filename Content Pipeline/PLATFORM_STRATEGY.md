# Platform strategy — where this actually gets posted, and why

**Status: first pass, grounded in general current platform mechanics.** A
dedicated research pass is running to sharpen the specifics (exact current
algorithm signals, niche case studies); this doc gets strengthened when that
lands. Usable now — don't wait on it to start posting.

## The recommendation, in order

1. **Instagram (Reels primary, carousels secondary) — the anchor.**
   Proven channel: real past organic engagement on this exact account, and
   social is already a top-2 traffic source most days per the daily digest
   (30-42% of sessions on many recent days) — that's a live revenue signal,
   not a hypothesis. Reels is the best format-market fit for the pipeline's
   strongest material: personalisation reveals, transformation, choice
   carousels.
2. **TikTok — co-equal, not an afterthought.** The brand's actual raw
   material (satisfying reveals, real workshop process, imperfect authentic
   footage) is exactly what TikTok's discovery favours. TikTok's ranking is
   more purely content-based and less follower-graph-dependent than
   Instagram — a single strong video can still find real reach even from a
   small account, which matters while this account's TikTok presence is
   young relative to its Instagram history.
3. **YouTube Shorts — add as a third distribution point, don't build for
   it separately.** Near-zero marginal cost: the same finished vertical
   asset, one more upload. The advantage over TikTok/Reels is longevity — a
   good Short keeps surfacing through YouTube's search and suggested layers
   for months, not just the first 48-72 hours. Cross-post validated winners
   (things that already proved themselves on TikTok/IG first); don't build
   YouTube-native content yet.
4. **Facebook — distribution layer via the same Reels asset, not a
   separate creative target.** Meta serves Reels across IG and Facebook
   from one upload. Facebook's audience skews toward the decision/occasion
   stage, which fits gifting deadlines and link-driven posts — exactly what
   `README.md`'s existing Facebook adaptation notes already say.

## YouTube long-form — verdict: not now

Different audience intent (deliberate search/subscribe vs scroll-discovery),
much higher production commitment per piece, and no existing channel or
subscriber base to build from. The brand's strength — fast, visual,
satisfying personalisation reveals — doesn't naturally stretch to 8-15
minute long-form without inventing a genuinely different content type (a
proper "inside the workshop" documentary series, for instance). Worth
revisiting as a deliberate future swing once the short-form system is
running smoothly and producing a real backlog of proof — not a near-term
priority.

## Format specs (working defaults — will tighten with research)

| Platform | Aspect | Sweet-spot length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 9:16 | 12-25s | Native, immediate; imperfect real footage often beats a polished edit |
| Instagram Reels | 9:16 | 7-15s hook-driven; up to ~30-60s for storytelling | Keep primary copy in the y=360-1430 safe zone (`README.md`) |

## What actually drives reach, per platform

- **TikTok:** near-total weight on whether people stay past the first
  second, then completion rate and rewatches. Shares matter more than
  likes. Trending audio still helps discovery but isn't load-bearing the
  way it was a few years ago — a strong native hook matters more.
- **Instagram Reels:** watch time and completion, but saves and shares are
  the two signals that push a Reel to non-followers — this is why
  "choice"/"guide" content (inherently saveable) earns reach that pure
  entertainment content doesn't.
- **YouTube Shorts:** similar completion-driven mechanics to TikTok, but
  sits inside a search-and-suggest ecosystem — a Short can surface from a
  text search months later in a way a TikTok/Reel essentially never does.
- **Facebook:** three-second view counts as a real metric here in a way it
  doesn't elsewhere; shares and comments carry real weight; link clicks
  matter more here than on any other platform in this list, because
  Facebook's audience is more likely to be occasion/deadline-driven and
  ready to act.

## Cross-posting: do it, but do it right

Posting the same finished master across all four platforms same-day is the
only sustainable model for a small team — the alternative (separate content
per platform) doesn't scale. What actually costs reach isn't cross-posting
itself, it's cross-posting *carelessly*:

- **Never leave another platform's watermark or UI baked into the export.**
  A visible TikTok watermark on an Instagram or YouTube upload is a real,
  well-documented reach penalty. The pipeline already produces a clean
  frozen master per `README.md`'s approval gates — always post from that,
  never a screen-recorded repost.
- **Always re-wrap the hook, on-screen text and caption per platform.**
  Same core asset, different opening seconds and different caption voice —
  this is already the house rule in `README.md`'s Platform Adaptation
  section; this doc doesn't change it, just adds YouTube to it.

## Cadence

Hold the existing weekly structure in `README.md` (4 core posts + 1
optional) — it's already well-designed for a small team. The change this
doc adds: treat every finished master as a 4-platform release (IG + TikTok
+ YouTube Shorts + Facebook), not a 1-platform post. Same production effort,
roughly 4x the distribution.
