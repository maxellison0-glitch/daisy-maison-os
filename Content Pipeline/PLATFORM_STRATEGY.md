# Platform strategy — where this actually gets posted, and why

Grounded in a dedicated research pass (30+ searches, primary sources
including Instagram's own official ranking post and TikTok's case-study
library) run 23 July 2026. Specific stats will drift as platforms iterate —
treat percentages as directional, prioritise this account's own 60-90 day
data once it exists over any benchmark here.

## The recommendation, in order

1. **Instagram Reels and TikTok — co-primary, run in parallel, not
   sequentially.**
   - *Instagram:* this account has real organic history — reactivating a
     known account is measurably cheaper than a cold start (most recover
     within 7-14 days of resumed consistent posting). Social is already a
     top-2 traffic source most days per the daily digest (30-42% of
     sessions) — a live revenue signal, not a hypothesis. Instagram's
     confirmed #1 discovery signal for reaching non-followers via Reels is
     **DM shares (sends-per-reach)** — weighted above watch time. This maps
     directly onto this product category: "send this to your bridesmaid,"
     "tag your fiancé" is a completely natural share trigger for a Mr & Mrs
     sign or personalised gift box in a way it isn't for most products.
   - *TikTok:* discovery is almost entirely merit-based, not follower-gated
     — new/dormant accounts cold-start to only ~200-500 viewers and scale
     purely on engagement, so this account isn't structurally disadvantaged
     there. TikTok also functions as a literal search engine for this exact
     buyer: 40-65% of Gen Z use it as a primary search tool, and wedding
     planning is a heavily search-and-compare purchase journey. The brand's
     actual raw material — reveal/unboxing, real workshop process — is
     TikTok's best-documented high-performing format for personalised
     physical products, not something to invent from scratch. Wedding-niche
     accounts specifically average ~12% follower growth and ~4.6%
     engagement on TikTok — genuine headroom in this exact niche.
   - Practical split: same shoot, different edits. TikTok leans into
     reveal/process/search-intent captions; Instagram leans into
     saveable/shareable choice content and shoppable tags.

2. **YouTube Shorts — real hedge, not a bespoke pillar.** Repurpose
   TikTok/Reels winners at near-zero marginal cost. Different discovery
   mechanism (search + suggested + feed, not just swipe) that compounds
   over months instead of decaying in 48-72 hours — genuine insurance
   against depending entirely on two algorithms this business doesn't
   control. Don't expect meaningful ad revenue (Shorts RPM is genuinely
   trivial, $0.01-0.07/1,000 views) — the value is traffic to profile/product,
   not the view itself.

3. **Facebook — auto-cross-post via Meta's native tool, no bespoke
   content.** Organic Page reach sits under 2% of followers on median, so
   this is not worth dedicated production. The audience case is real
   (Facebook's UK strength is genuinely 35-54 — mother-of-the-bride,
   teacher-gift buyers, grandparents — directly part of this buying group)
   but the honest framing is distribution bonus, not a content engine.

## YouTube long-form — verdict: not now, define the trigger

Genuinely promising later, specifically for this brand: craftsmanship
process content ("how a personalised wedding sign is actually made") is an
unusually strong long-form fit compared to most e-commerce categories, and
long-form YouTube's value compounds on a much longer window than short-form
(comparable brand case: 60%+ of conversions from a long-form campaign
landed weeks or months after upload). But it's the most resource-heavy
format by a wide margin, and without an existing subscriber base or a
warmed-up Shorts funnel feeding it, a long-form upload has no distribution
path — it sits at low views for months regardless of quality.

**Concrete trigger to revisit, not a vague "later":** once TikTok/Reels are
posting consistently for ~2-3 months and there's a natural backlog of
workshop footage being shot anyway for short-form, stitch that raw footage
into a first 8-12 minute cut as a near-zero-incremental-cost experiment.
Judge by whether it picks up search-driven view growth in its second and
third month post-upload before investing in dedicated long-form production.

## What actually drives reach, per platform

- **TikTok:** watch-time-per-impression is the primary signal (not raw
  views), then completion/rewatch rate (~70%+ completion is roughly the
  "viral" bar now), then shares — with DM-shares weighted above public
  shares as a stronger endorsement signal — then comments (now weighted
  above likes, with substantive comments counted more than emoji). TikTok
  also optimises for total session length, favouring content that keeps
  someone scrolling rather than judging each video in isolation.
- **Instagram Reels:** Instagram has officially moved away from one
  algorithm — Feed, Stories, Reels and Explore each rank differently.
  Confirmed top signals for Reels specifically: watch time, DM shares
  (sends-per-reach — the strongest lever for reaching non-followers), and
  likes-per-reach. Instagram explicitly down-ranks: watermarked Reels,
  muted/bordered Reels, majority-text Reels, and Reels already posted
  elsewhere on Instagram (literal reposts).
- **YouTube Shorts:** distribution runs through four separate paths (Shorts
  feed, Search, suggested/"Up Next," channel page) versus TikTok's
  essentially single swipe-feed. Rewards a high % of duration watched
  (~85%+) more than raw completion, and — structurally important — Shorts
  and long-form performance are now decoupled: posting Shorts doesn't help
  or hurt long-form eligibility.
- **Facebook:** Reels ranking (shares, saves, completions) is now genuinely
  different from Feed ranking (comments-per-reach, conversation quality) —
  Facebook has bifurcated into an "entertainment feed" and a "conversation
  feed" with separate logics.

## An operational tool worth using: Trial Reels

Instagram's Trial Reels feature tests a Reel with only non-followers first;
if it performs within 72 hours, it auto-shares to followers. Genuinely
useful here — it lets hooks and formats get tested without risking the
existing follower base's feed experience. Worth building into the
production workflow as a default for anything experimental, not just
knowing it exists.

## Cross-posting: confirmed safe, mechanics matter more than timing

No platform can detect or penalise cross-posting the same underlying video
elsewhere — Instagram's own ranking post confirms this. The entire penalty
mechanism is about **visible artifacts on the file**, not the act of
cross-posting:

- **A visible TikTok watermark on an Instagram/YouTube upload gets that
  specific upload actively suppressed** — estimates range 50-70% reach
  reduction. The pipeline already produces a clean frozen master per
  `README.md`'s approval gates — always post from that, never a
  screen-recorded repost.
- **TikTok-original commercial audio is frequently unlicensed for Meta
  platforms** — a straight cross-post can get muted or capped for the audio
  alone, independent of the watermark issue. Re-select audio per platform.
- Same-day posting across platforms doesn't hurt performance on any of them
  — there's no shared-timing penalty. Staggering by a few hours is an
  editorial choice (react to which hook performs first), not an
  algorithmic requirement.
- **Always re-wrap the hook, on-screen text and caption per platform** —
  already the house rule in `README.md`'s Platform Adaptation section;
  this doc adds YouTube to it, doesn't change it.

## Cadence

| Platform | Cadence |
|---|---|
| TikTok | 4-6x/week for the first ~60 days (rebuilding algorithmic trust), settling to 3-4x/week sustaining |
| Instagram Reels | 3-5x/week, Reels prioritised over static/carousel by default (2.7% engagement vs 1.3-1.4%) |
| YouTube Shorts | Same cadence as TikTok/Reels, but hand-picked winners only — not a 1:1 auto-repost of everything |
| Facebook | Auto-cross-posted from Instagram, zero extra production |
| YouTube long-form | None yet — see trigger condition above |

This account is a **relaunch with real history**, not a cold start — treat
the first 3-4 weeks as a deliberate consistency-rebuilding sprint on both
TikTok and Instagram together (near-daily posting, active engagement on the
account's own comments) to re-signal "active account" to both platforms'
trust systems, then settle to the sustaining cadence above.

## AI-generated content: disclosure is now a real, enforced requirement

Directly relevant to Freya (`Personas/FREYA.md`,
`Personas/HIGGSFIELD_CHARACTER_BUILD.md`) — this changes from "worth
watching" to "concrete operational requirement":

- **TikTok's 2026 Synthetic and Manipulated Media policy requires visible
  labels on AI-generated or significantly-altered content depicting
  realistic people**, enforced with automated detection and a four-tier
  penalty system: warning → reduced distribution → removal → account-level
  action. Meta auto-flags AI content on Instagram/Facebook and supports
  manual labelling too.
- **The good news: proper labelling does not appear to cost reach**, per
  TikTok's own guidance — a proactively labelled post performs normally as
  long as it otherwise meets community guidelines.
- **The real risk is quality-based suppression, not disclosure-based
  suppression.** Over 60% of analysed TikTok transcripts contained
  AI-generated text in early 2026, and TikTok has adjusted For You Page
  weighting to specifically deprioritise content its synthetic-media
  quality classifier flags — uncanny motion, rendering artifacts, generic
  templated patterns — independent of whether it's labelled. This is a
  live, shifting part of the landscape worth re-checking quarterly, not a
  settled rule.

**What this means for Freya specifically:** a well-produced, high-retention
AI-assisted video is not systematically punished; a generic, uncanny,
obviously-templated one increasingly is. This is exactly what
`PUBLISH_READINESS.md` dimension 4 (brand tone / no AI spectacle) already
exists to catch — the quality bar this repo already holds itself to is the
same bar that keeps content out of classifier suppression. Two concrete
requirements this adds, not previously specified: (1) use each platform's
AI-content disclosure setting on anything featuring Freya before it
publishes — this is now a compliance step, not optional; (2) her current
build stays non-talking/non-lip-synced (already the Phase 1 recommendation
in `HIGGSFIELD_CHARACTER_BUILD.md`) — realistic talking-head AI video is
exactly the category facing the tightest platform scrutiny right now.

One credible alternative worth putting in front of Max directly, since it's
his call: **lean Freya toward an overtly stylised character rather than
photorealistic** — more presenter/mascot-format than an attempt at
photorealistic human mimicry. This sidesteps the disclosure/classifier risk
zone entirely rather than managing it, at the cost of some of the warmth a
believable real-feeling persona gives. The recommendation here is to keep
building the photorealistic route already underway (it's working, per the
Stage 2 evidence in `Creative Studio/active/FREYA-character-build/`) with
disclosure treated as a mandatory pre-publish step — but this is a real
fork, not a foregone conclusion, and it's cheap to change direction now
versus after a Soul identity is trained.

## Real case studies worth knowing

- **Little Moons** (UK mochi ice cream) — the clearest UK small-brand
  TikTok case study that exists. Organic user reveal/reaction videos (not
  brand-made) drove sales up over 2,000% and tripled brand awareness; the
  brand is reinvesting £2m into TikTok/creator-led marketing in 2026, four
  years on — a real validation that the channel kept paying off. The
  mechanism (an inherently reveal-able, reaction-worthy physical product,
  amplified by real reactions) is close to identical to what this pipeline
  is already built to produce.
- **Made By Mitchell** (UK beauty) — founder-led daily TikTok LIVEs while
  packing orders, surprise freebies, became the first UK beauty business to
  hit $1M in a single TikTok Shop live day. Directly relevant to Max's
  persona specifically: founder-personality, real process, product
  demonstration — not a scripted ad.
- **Partner in Wine** and similar UK small brands passed 60K TikTok
  followers on native product-demo content alone, no paid media — evidence
  the "no big budget needed" thesis holds at small-brand UK scale, not just
  for large US brands.
